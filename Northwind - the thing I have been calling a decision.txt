NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
The thing I have been calling a decision

Prepared by:  Priya Raghunathan
Date:         12 March 2018
Status:       Internal
Read by:      Alice, Wes, Marcus, John


Marcus wrote up 17 February and asked for two things.  I agree with both.  This
is about a third one that is mine and that nobody asked for.


WHAT WE AGREED IN JUNE

When Wes took the tools and I took the customers, I set two conditions.

    He does not change a check that is currently catching something without
    telling me first.

    A change to a customer's scripts does not go out until I say it goes out.

Both were reasonable.  I would set them again.  I want to be exact about what
has happened to the second one in nine months.


THE NUMBERS

Between 19 June 2017 and 28 February 2018, Wes asked me forty-one times whether
a change to a customer script could go out.

I said no four times.

Of those four: two were Summit inside a selling window, one was Hartwell in the
week Cheryl was on leave and there was nobody there to telephone if it went
wrong, and one was Brightwater, where the change was fine and the timing was
not because they were closing the quarter.

    Median time from Wes asking to me answering: four hours.
    Longest: three days.  I was in Colorado Springs with Ruth.
    Thirty-seven of the forty-one answers were yes.


WHAT I SAID NO ABOUT

Read the four again.  Not one of them is a technical judgement.  Not one of
them is a judgement at all.

They are four facts about four customers.

    Summit does not take changes during a selling window.
    Hartwell does not take changes when Cheryl is away.
    Brightwater does not take changes in the last week of a quarter.
    Clear Creek takes changes whenever, and Ruth wants to be told after.

I know those four facts.  I have known them for years.  They are not written
anywhere.


THE PART THAT IS MINE

I have been calling this a decision.

It is not a decision.  It is a lookup, and I am the table.

Wes has waited a median of four hours, forty-one times, for me to read a
sentence off a table that exists only in my head.  That is something close to a
week of his time and none of it bought anybody anything, because I said yes
thirty-seven times and the four noes were facts he could have read himself if
anyone had written them down.

I want to be honest about why they were not written down, because "we were busy"
is not the reason and I have had five years.

    The records I keep are the best thing I have made here.  I built them so
    that nobody would have to hold a company in their head.  And I left the one
    part out that was holding me in it.

    I do not think I did that deliberately.  I am fairly sure I did not do it
    accidentally either.

That is the thing I am telling you.  Not that I am overloaded -- I am, and that
is ordinary and it is not interesting.  That we built a role out of a bottleneck
and I let everybody call it ownership, including me, and it has been that way
since about 2013.


WHAT CHANGES

1.  Every customer record gets a CHANGES heading.  What that customer will and
    will not take, when, and who has to be told after.  The four facts above and
    whatever else comes out when I write the other two.

    Same rules as every other heading.  Dated when it was last actually checked
    with the customer, not when somebody read it.  Empty means we have not
    established it, and empty means ask me, which is where we are now.

    Adding a heading means touching all the records, which I did on Saturday,
    and there are five of them: Summit, Brightwater, Hartwell, Clear Creek,
    Lindqvist.  That is an afternoon and it should have been an afternoon in
    2013.

    It is also five records and seven customers.  Dev Arora and Dr Ma have been
    with us since 2016 and neither of them has a record.  I found that out on
    Saturday by counting, having written the README in 2011 that says we keep
    one file per customer.  It is on the deferred list with my name on it and
    an April date, which is better than it was and is not the same as fixed.

2.  A customer-scripts change goes out when the record says it can.  Nobody
    asks me.  If the record is empty or the change is outside what it says,
    then ask, and the answer gets written into the record so that the next
    person does not have to.

    I would rather be told afterwards than asked beforehand.  nwpush now writes
    a line saying who pushed what and when, so being told afterwards is not a
    thing anybody has to remember to do.

3.  The June review condition stays, with one change: it does not have to be
    me.  It has to be somebody who is not the author, and they have to run
    nwtry and put the output in the commit, which is already the rule.

    February and October both say the same thing about this.  Two people from
    the same small company reading the same change find the same things and
    miss the same things.  The review is worth having and it is worth having
    from whoever is nearest.  Making it me made it slow without making it
    better.

4.  Wes goes on the telephone rota from 1 April.

    Alice took him off the list in November 2012 because he was new.  He has
    not been new since about 2014 and nobody moved him back, including me, and
    I was the one keeping the rota.

    Marcus has now said this twice in writing, in 2016 and again last month,
    and I did not act on it either time.  He put it better than I will:
    you find out what you have built when it wakes you up.


WHAT DOES NOT CHANGE

I am still the person a customer telephones.  That is not a bottleneck, that is
the job, and Laura has had my number for seven years.

I am still the person who decides what a record says.  A CHANGES line is not a
note anybody can add; it is a claim about a customer and it gets checked with
the customer.

And nobody sends anything to Summit in a selling window, ever, for any reason,
including me.
