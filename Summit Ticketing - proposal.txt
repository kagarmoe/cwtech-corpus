WHAT WE UNDERSTAND ABOUT YOUR SITUATION

You have two leased Linux servers at Mile High Hosting. One of them runs your
application and your database. The second was added last year and is doing
several unrelated jobs.

Mile High supplies the hardware, the network and the addresses. They will
replace a failed server and reboot one on request. They do not administer the
operating system and they do not look after your application. Nobody currently
does that on a defined basis.

Mark has been doing it because there was nobody else, and he has told you he
would rather go back to development.

In March, customers could not complete purchases for several hours. Mile High
reported both servers reachable. Mark was unavailable. Nobody has established
what actually went wrong, and there are no records from that day that would let
anyone establish it now.

That last point matters more than the outage itself. We do not want to be in
that position again, and neither do you.


PART ONE -- INITIAL ENVIRONMENT ENGAGEMENT

Fixed price: $2,400

Before we can take responsibility for your systems, we need to know what they
are. This part is a finite piece of work with an end.

    We inventory both servers and everything running on them, and write it
    down.

    We identify every user account on both machines and establish with you and
    Mark who still needs access. We do not remove anything without Mark
    reviewing the list first -- some of those accounts may belong to scripts.

    We record the accounts, providers and credentials we would need in an
    outage, including your registrar, your DNS host and your Mile High
    account. Right now some of that exists only in Mark's memory.

    We put monitoring on the services we are agreeing to manage. Today the
    only monitoring is a check that the servers answer a ping and a check that
    the home page loads, and the alerts go to Mark's personal email. Nothing
    watches your database, your disk space, your scheduled jobs, or whether
    last night's backup actually ran.

    We restore your database from a backup onto a separate machine and confirm
    it works.

That last item is the one I would ask you not to cut. Your nightly backups are
running and the files are there. Nobody has ever restored one. Until somebody
does, you have backup files rather than a backup, and there is no way to know
which until the day it matters.

If we find something during this work that changes what ongoing management
should cost, we will tell you before Part Two starts rather than afterward.


PART TWO -- ONGOING MANAGEMENT

$1,200 per month, beginning when Part One is complete.

We take operational responsibility for:

    the operating system on both servers
    Apache
    MySQL availability and routine database administration
    disk space and storage
    backups, including a restore test on a regular basis
    monitoring of the services above
    coordinating hardware problems with Mile High
    first response when Summit reports that the service is not working

Routine work is included. Work that takes more than an hour, we will tell you
about and quote before we start it. New server builds, migrations and
application deployments are quoted separately regardless of how long they take.


WHEN SOMETHING BREAKS

    Monday to Friday, 8:00 to 18:00 Mountain, we will respond within one hour.

    Outside those hours you will have a number that reaches us directly. We
    will answer it when we can. We are not going to put a time against that,
    because we would be making it up.

    We do not promise how long a repair will take, at any hour. What takes ten
    minutes and what takes six hours look identical when the phone rings.

I would rather tell you this now than have you discover it during an incident.
We are two people. A larger provider can promise you a rota. We can promise
that when you call, you are talking to somebody who knows your systems.


THE LINE BETWEEN US AND MARK

This is the part I want to be clearest about, because it is where these
arrangements usually go wrong.

We do not take responsibility for whether your application is correct. If it is
running and it charges the wrong price for a ticket, that is a development
problem and it belongs to Mark.

But if your customers cannot buy tickets and nobody knows why, that is ours
until we have established whether the problem is infrastructure or application.
Then it goes where it belongs.

You should not have to work out which kind of problem you have before you are
allowed to call us. In March you could not have. That is exactly the gap we
intend to cover.


WHAT WE NEED FROM SUMMIT

    Administrative access to both servers for Alice.

    Your registrar, DNS and Mile High account details recorded somewhere we
    can reach during an outage.

    Mark to send a short note to a Northwind address before he changes
    anything on either server. Not approval -- notice. If we spend time
    chasing a failure caused by a change we did not know about, that time is
    billable outside the monthly fee.

    A decision from you and Mark on the several user accounts Alice found that
    nobody could identify.


WHAT THIS PROPOSAL DOES NOT INCLUDE

    Moving your servers. We see no reason to move them and are not proposing
    it.

    Rebuilding or redesigning your application.

    Fixing WEB2. Alice's view is that it is doing four unrelated jobs and
    doing them badly, but it is working and it is not urgent. We would like to
    understand which of those jobs you actually need before anyone changes it.
    We are not proposing that work now.

    Anything to do with your office network or desktops.
