NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
What we decided about the cage

Prepared by:  John Mercer
Reviewed by:  Alice Chen, Marcus Reyes
Date:         18 November 2013
Status:       Internal


THE DECISION

We are taking a cage at Alder Street.  Not a building.  Not a hundred kilowatts.

    40 kW, contracted.  Room in the cage for about 70 if the facility will
    sell us more power later, which they say they will and which is not in
    writing.

    Two years, with a one-year break at our option.

    We do not sign a term longer than the customers behind it.  That was
    Alice's condition and it is now the rule.

Against that we have signed Halvard for 30 kW on two years, which is the
customer commitment that pays for the room, and Lindqvist moves in from his
cabinet at 9 kW.  That is 39 of 40 on day one.

Petra negotiated hard.  She has a remedy if we fail to deliver power, which is
the first time anyone has had a remedy against us for anything, and she is
right to have it.


WHY NOT MORE

Petra asked for a hundred.  I wanted to say yes.

Marcus's arithmetic: at a hundred kilowatts we would be committing to power we
could not fill from anyone else if Halvard left, in a facility that would have
to upgrade a room for us, on a five year term because that is what pays for
their upgrade.

Alice: "If we sign five years of power against two years of customers, we have
not taken a business risk.  We have taken a bet, with a landlord as the
counterparty."

So we told Petra forty now and more later if she extends.  She was not offended.
She said she had expected us to say a hundred and had been prepared to argue us
down, and that our version was more sensible than hers.

I am recording that because it is the second time this year that being honest
about what we cannot do has won us work.


WHY A CAGE AND NOT A BUILDING

Alice's test from March: a room that is still worth having if every one of these
machines goes in a skip.

A cage at Alder Street passes.  It is a facility we would put Summit in.  If
mining goes away we have 40 kW of ordinary colocation in a building we already
use, which is an asset and not an albatross.

A shed on a ranch with a substation would be cheaper per kilowatt and would fail
that test completely.  Two of the people I spoke to this autumn are doing
exactly that.


WHAT WE HAVE NOT SETTLED

**Concentration.**  Alice asked what percentage of revenue we are willing to
have in mining, and asked it before we are it rather than after.

I did not want to answer and she made me.  We agreed no number.  What we agreed
instead: we look at it every quarter, and if mining passes half of revenue we
stop taking mining customers until it does not, regardless of what is on offer.

I do not like it.  I agreed to it because I could not explain why I did not
like it in any way that survived being said out loud.

**Density.**  Marcus's objection stands and is unresolved.  We are building for
what exists now.  If the machines get much denser, 40 kW of power in this cage
becomes the wrong shape rather than the wrong size.  He wants that written down
so nobody is surprised.  It is now written down.

**What happens if Halvard leaves at two years.**  We have a year of break
clause and a facility we can fill with ordinary customers slowly.  That is not
a plan.  It is a cushion.


WHAT I SAID IN MARCH

That I was keen, and that somebody should tell me if I was keen again.

Alice told me.  Then she said she was for it anyway, with conditions, and the
conditions are what made this a decision rather than an enthusiasm.
