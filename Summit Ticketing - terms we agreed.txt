NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
Summit Ticketing -- terms we agreed

Prepared by:  John Mercer
Reviewed by:  Alice Chen
Date:         April 22, 2010
Status:       Internal


PURPOSE

Alice sent five questions with her environment review on the 19th and told me
not to promise Laura anything until we had answered them. We went through them
Tuesday and Wednesday.

This is what we agreed, written down so that what I put in front of Laura and
what Alice has to deliver afterward are the same thing.


1. THE INITIAL WORK IS SEPARATE, AND IT COMES FIRST

Alice will not take responsibility for servers she has not inventoried, for
backups nobody has restored, or for services nobody is watching. I am not going
to argue her out of that, and I would not want to sell against it.

My concern was commercial. I do not want to hand Laura a monthly agreement with
three weeks of setup work buried inside it. If something breaks in week two we
will have spent the margin before we have earned any of it.

Agreed: we quote the initial work separately, at a fixed price, and monthly
management does not start until it is done.

    Initial environment engagement -- $2,400, fixed

    - inventory both servers and everything running on them
    - identify every user account and establish who still needs access
    - record the accounts, providers and credentials we would need during
      an outage, including DNS and the Mile High account
    - put monitoring on the services we are agreeing to manage
    - restore the database from a backup and confirm it works

The restore is the part I would have been tempted to skip and the part Alice
says is not optional. Her words: until somebody restores one, they have backup
files, not a backup.

Two things this also does for us. Laura gets a small, bounded, finite piece of
work to say yes to instead of an open-ended monthly commitment from a company
that is six weeks old. And if the environment turns out to be worse than Alice
thinks, we find out while we are being paid to look, not after we have promised
to run it.


2. WHAT "WE RESPOND" MEANS

    Monday to Friday, 8:00 to 18:00 Mountain -- we respond within one hour.

    Outside those hours -- there is a number that reaches us. We will answer
    it when we can. We are not putting a time against it.

    At no hour do we promise when something will be fixed.

Alice's reasoning, which I am recording because I expect to be tempted to
soften it in front of a customer: we can tell Laura when we have picked the
problem up. We cannot tell her when it will be fixed, because that depends
entirely on what broke. Promising a repair time is how two people end up
failing an agreement they signed.

The after-hours number is Alice's mobile. We both know that is not a rota and
it is not a plan. It is what we have. If Summit signs and we sign anybody else,
this is the first thing that breaks.


3. APPLICATION FAILURES

Agreed, as Alice proposed it on the 19th:

    We do not take responsibility for whether the application is correct. If
    it is running and it charges the wrong price for a ticket, that is Mark's.

    If customers cannot use the service and nobody knows why, that is ours
    until we have established whether the failure is infrastructure or
    application. Then it goes where it belongs.

We cannot ask Laura to work out which kind of problem she has before she is
allowed to call us. She does not know, and if she did she would not need us.

This is the thing I have been trying to sell since March and could not say
cleanly. It is now the sentence I use.


4. WHAT THE MONTHLY FEE COVERS

Routine work is included. Anything that is not routine, we quote before we
start.

We could not find a clean definition, so we are using time:

    Work under an hour -- included.

    Work over an hour -- we tell Laura first and quote it.

    Never included regardless of time: new server builds, migrations,
    application deployments, anything that changes what the environment is
    rather than keeping it running.

I am not confident this holds. An hour is easy to explain and easy to argue
about, and I can already think of a half-hour job I would not want to do ten
times a month for nothing. But we do not have enough history to draw the line
anywhere better, and I would rather have a crude line we can revisit than a
clever one we cannot explain to Laura.


5. MARK

We cannot stop Mark changing production. He has root, he built the
application, and he is Laura's contractor, not ours. Asking Laura to take his
access away would make us the problem.

Agreed:

    We ask Mark to send a note to a Northwind address before he changes
    anything on either server. Not approval. Notice.

    If he does not, and we spend time chasing a failure he caused, that time
    is billable outside the monthly fee.

    We say this to Laura in the proposal rather than discovering it during
    the first argument.

Alice wanted it in writing because she expects it to happen. I think she is
right. Mark has been the only person touching those machines for two years and
he is not going to change that habit because we sent him an email.


6. PRICE

    Initial engagement -- $2,400, fixed
    Monthly management -- $1,200

The monthly number is for Summit. It is not a rate card.

We still have not answered the question I raised on the 12th: what the
management fee should actually be based on. Per server is easy to say and I am
not convinced it measures anything. Summit has two servers, and WEB1 is most of
the work while WEB2 is mostly a nuisance. Charging the same for both would be
wrong in one direction or the other.

For now this is a number that reflects what Alice thinks the work is, plus
enough that we are not doing it for nothing. We will have a better answer after
we have run it for a few months. We should not turn this figure into a model
before then.


7. WHAT WE STILL DO NOT HAVE

A services agreement. We cannot sign Summit without one and I do not want to
use something I found on the internet. I am getting one drawn up this week. The
terms above are what it has to say.


WHAT I AM SENDING LAURA

A proposal covering the initial engagement and the monthly management, written
in the language above. She gets the boundary and the after-hours limits in
writing, up front, rather than finding them out in an incident.

Alice has read this and agrees it matches what she will actually deliver.
