CAIRNWELL TECHNOLOGIES
Configuration management, asked properly

Prepared by:  Wes Okafor
Date:         4 March 2019
Status:       Internal


If we sign five years we are saying we will run this with six people until
2025.  I asked what that costs and this is the part of the answer that is mine.

Alice asked me to look at configuration management, which she has turned down
twice, and to do it without either of us leaning on the fact that she turned it
down.  Her words: "I said no in 2013 and 2015 and neither of those is a reason
in 2019.  Go and find out."


WHAT I LOOKED AT

Chef.  I looked at Puppet and Ansible enough to know what they are and then
stopped, because if we do this it is going to be Chef and I would rather spend
the time on one thing properly.

    Why Chef and not the others is not a long argument.  It is Ruby, Priya and
    I both read Ruby, the run model is a thing that converges a machine to a
    described state and reports what it changed, and it is the one I have
    actually used.  Wes at a previous employer is not a recommendation but it
    is not nothing either.


THE PROBLEM IT WOULD SOLVE, WHICH IS REAL AND IS SEVEN YEARS OLD

    Brightwater: bw-web1's cw-cleanup was   WO      Jan 2013    Nov 2012
      edited on the machine by unknown
      cwpush -n still reports DIFFERS.

That is on the deferred list in Priya's handwriting and it has been there since
before I could push to a customer machine.

cwpush can see that a file on a customer's machine is not the file in our
repository.  It has been able to see it since Alice wrote it in July 2012.  It
cannot do anything about it unless a person runs it with the right argument and
reads the output.

    Somebody edited a file on bw-web1 in November 2012.  We have known ever
    since.  It is still different.

That is the argument for convergence and it is ours, not an industry's.


WHAT I GOT WRONG BEFORE I STARTED

I sat down to write that Chef will not run on most of our machines, because
most of our machines are out of support, and I had the table half typed before
I checked it.

    web1.summit     CentOS 5.4      out of support March 2017
    web2.summit     CentOS 5.3      out of support March 2017
    bw-web1         CentOS 5.5      out of support March 2017
    bw-db1          CentOS 5.5      out of support March 2017
    ccdata1         Debian 5        out of support February 2012
    ccdata2         Debian 5        out of support February 2012
    ccweb1          Debian 5        out of support February 2012
    hr-www          Ubuntu 8.04     out of support May 2013
    mon1            Debian 8
    sl-node01       Ubuntu 14.04    Dev's; we have a login for the scratch disk

Out of support is a fact about the vendor.  Whether a program runs on a machine
is a fact about the machine.  They are not the same fact and I had let the
first one stand in for the second because it did not need an argument.

What I found when I read the download site and the documentation instead of
the calendar:

    **The Chef package brings its own Ruby.**  It does not use the one on the
    machine.  So "these machines have never had the Ruby it wants" is not a
    reason for anything.  The only question is whether a package was built for
    the release.

    **Chef 14**, this year's, is built for Debian 8 and Ubuntu 14.04.  That is
    mon1, and Dev's two scratch nodes, which are not ours to configure.  So the
    "two of seven" I was about to write was one.

    **CentOS 5** is gone from the current list.  It is on the 2017 list.  Chef
    12 was built for it, the last 12 came out in May of last year, and the
    packages are still on their site.  Nobody at Chef will answer a question
    about it.  It installs.

    **Debian 5 and Ubuntu 8.04.**  No package I can find, of any version.  The
    older Chefs, 10 and 11, were ordinary Ruby gems that ran on Ruby 1.8.7,
    which is what lenny ships.  Hardy ships 1.8.6, so hr-www would want a Ruby
    built by hand first.  Chef 11 has been dead for four years and 10 for
    longer.  I have not tried either and I am not going to try them on a
    customer's machine.  I believe they would run.

    **None of it needs a server.**  Since Chef 11 the client can run against a
    directory of cookbooks on the machine itself.  The way that directory
    would get onto a machine is cwpush, which we have.

So the honest table is not two of seven.  It is:

    mon1                                  Chef 14          supported
    web1, web2, bw-web1, bw-db1           Chef 12          unsupported since last year; installs
    ccdata1, ccdata2, ccweb1              Chef 10 or 11    dead upstream; a gem on the stock Ruby; untried
    hr-www                                the same         after building a Ruby

Three Chefs, ten years apart at the ends, on nine machines.  It runs.  "It will
not run" was the wrong reason, and I want that on paper because I expect
somebody to reach for it again, possibly me.

I tried the one that cost nothing.  On Friday afternoon I put Chef 14 on mon1
in local mode with a cookbook that manages one file.  It converged, it said
what it changed, and it took under two hours including the reading.  It is
still there.  I have not committed the cookbook, because it manages one file
that says hello and a cookbook in the repository would look like a decision.


WHAT THE ANSWER ACTUALLY TURNS ON

The question I should have started with: converge what, for whom.

**The eight we manage for other people.**  They have not changed since 2012.
Brightwater's cleanup script is the most recent thing anybody edited on one and
that was the edit we did not know about.  Their customers are on thirty days'
notice, all four of them, and nobody has ever paid us to upgrade one.  The risk
on those machines is the age of the operating system, and Chef does not touch
that.  What it would converge is a handful of scripts and cron lines that
cwdrift now looks at every Sunday.

    To do it I would run three vintages of the tool and test every cookbook on
    each, which is the cwtry lesson again with three axes instead of one.  And
    installing an unsupported agent on an unsupported machine that belongs to a
    customer is a change, with notice, in Dale's windows, for a benefit no
    customer asked for.

That is a no.  It is not "cannot".  It is "could, for nothing".

**The machines that are not ours.**  Dev's, and Fourmile's if we get Fourmile.
We rack them and power them and put hands on them and we do not configure
them.  It says so on the customer page and Dinesh said it twice to John.

    Configuration management for machines we are contractually not allowed to
    configure is not a small version of the project.  It is not the project.

**The machines that are ours, which I left out of my first draft and should
not have.**  Fourteen genomics nodes.  We bought them, in two lots, and we run
them -- "we run the machine, what it computes is theirs" is our own page.  They
are not in cwtry.hosts, which says a new release goes in the same week.  They
have no record; that has been on the deferred list since March 2018.  What
release they run is written on them and nowhere else.

    This is the only thing in the company that looks like a fleet: a number of
    machines that are supposed to be the same.  That is what the tool is for,
    and the eleven-hosts-with-shell-scripts argument was never about the
    eleven.  It was about these.

And then Alice's rule, which is the one I keep coming back to: do not commit
longer than the commitment behind it.  Dr Ma's grant ended in September.  She is
paying month to month and Alice is talking to her this month.  Rebuilding
fourteen nodes under a tool is a commitment of my time to machines that may not
have a customer in April.


SO, THIS YEAR

No, for the estate we manage for other people -- on the ground that it would
cost three vintages of the tool to converge machines nobody has changed in
seven years, for customers who can leave in thirty days.  Not on the ground
that it cannot be done.

Nothing, for machines that are not ours.

Yes, the size of what we own, when there is a term behind it.  mon1 already,
as a trial.  The genomics nodes the month Dr Ma signs, if she signs, and they
are the first real job.  And every machine we buy from now on under "our
machines" gets built from a cookbook in this repository rather than converged
to one afterwards, because a machine we are building is the one time nothing
has to be put right.  It only has to be described.

    That is not adoption and I am not going to call it adoption.  It is a
    trigger with a name on it, on the list, so that the day somebody signs the
    question has already been answered.


WHAT I AM DOING INSTEAD, AFTER GETTING IT WRONG ONCE

I was going to add a --converge option to cwpush, so that where it prints
DIFFERS it could put the file back.

Then I read cwpush properly, which I should have done first.

**It already does that.**  Alice wrote it in July 2012 and it has always worked
that way: -n shows you the diff and changes nothing, and without -n it copies
the file.  A push to Brightwater any time in the last seven years would have
put bw-web1 right.

So the gap is not that the tool cannot fix drift.  The gap is that **nobody runs
it unless they are changing something**, and we have not changed Brightwater's
cleanup script since 2012, so nobody has looked.

    That is a better answer than the one I was going to write and it is smaller.
    The tool is not missing a feature.  It is missing a reason to be run.

cwdrift.  Twenty lines.  It runs cwpush -n for every customer, prints only what
differs, and says nothing at all when everything matches.  In cron on a Sunday,
mailing Priya and me.

    Silence when it is fine, which is the same rule as cwcheck and for the same
    reason: a weekly mail that is usually empty gets read, and a weekly mail
    that always has something in it does not.

    We have known since 2011 that a check nobody acts on is not monitoring.
    This is not monitoring either -- it is a reminder to look -- and I would
    rather say what it is.

bw-web1 is fixed.  Seven years.  I read the diff first and it was a debug line
somebody added and forgot, and it had been doing nothing at all.

That is most of what Chef would have given us on the managed eight, for two
days instead of two months, on a machine from 2009.


WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER

I am writing this down so the next person does not have to have the argument
from the beginning, and so that nobody quotes this document in 2022 as though
it settled something.

    A signed term on machines that are ours.  Those machines, under Chef, the
    same month.  This one has a name on it and it may be April.
    A managed customer who pays for a rebuild.  A rebuilt machine gets it with
    the rebuild; I am not converging a 2009 machine and I would build a 2019
    one no other way.
    Managed work at a scale where nine hosts becomes fifty.
    Somebody besides me who can change a script.

Any one of those and this is worth asking again.  Two of them and I would
expect to be told to do it rather than asked.

    I do not think we should adopt a tool because the industry has.  I also do
    not think "we manage nine hosts with shell scripts in 2019" is a sentence
    I would enjoy saying to somebody I was trying to hire.  The one I would
    enjoy less is "we said it could not run, and nobody checked."

    -- WO

    argument now and I would have used them anyway.

    Wes's first draft said it could not run on our machines, and I would have

    sounds like an engineer's and is a calendar's.  Where he has landed is
