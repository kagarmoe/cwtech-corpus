WHAT I BUILT, AND WHY IT IS NOT MINING-SPECIFIC

Alice's second assumption is that somebody else would take this room.  My job
was to make that true, and here is what it cost.

    12 kW a rack, not 8.  We need 8 today.  Twelve buys one generation of
    density and not two, and I want that written down as a limit and not a
    boast.

    Hot aisle containment, not in-row units per cabinet.  More expensive up
    front, and it does not care what is in the racks.  Per-cabinet cooling
    tuned to mining rigs would have been cheaper and would have made the room
    worth less to anybody else.

    PDUs are the only mining-specific part and they are movable.  A suite is a
    suite.

    Everything to code, by a licensed contractor, inspected, with the
    paperwork in the file.  I have watched two people run rooms on extension
    leads and a prayer.  Both of them were fine right up until they were not.

If every mining customer disappeared tomorrow, what we own is a compliant
300 kW suite with containment in a facility people want to be in.  That is the
asset Alice is betting on and I have built it so she is not wrong about it.


WHAT THIS MEANS OPERATIONALLY

    Temperature.  We now have a room and not a cabinet.  I have put sensors on
    the hot and cold aisles and Priya has a check reading them.  We have never
    monitored a temperature in four years.

    Power.  Per-cabinet draw from the PDUs, plus suite total against the
    180 kW floor and the 300 kW ceiling.  I want to know before the facility
    tells me.

    Remote hands is now a service and not a favour.  Four customers, four
    hundred and something machines by summer.  Somebody is going to be in that
    suite most days.

    That somebody cannot always be me.  This is assumption five in Alice's
    document and it is the one I would fix first.  We should hire before we
    are desperate, and I have said that in writing now so that it is not a
    surprise in July.
