Laura,

The initial engagement is complete. Alice worked on your systems from May 10 to
May 14. This is what we found and what is now in place.

The short version: your systems are in better shape than I expected and your
backups are in worse shape than you believed. Nothing is on fire.


WHAT IS NOW IN PLACE

You have an inventory. Before this there was not one. We know what is on both
machines, what runs on them, what depends on what, and who has access.

You have monitoring. Since May 13 we have been checking both servers every five
minutes -- disk space, the database, your home page, and whether last night's
backup actually appeared. Alerts come to us, not to Mark's personal email.

You have a restore that somebody has actually performed.

Access is tidied. Eleven accounts existed across the two machines. One belonged
to a contractor who finished in early 2009 and still had administrative access;
that is disabled, with your agreement. The rest we identified and left alone,
including two that looked disposable and were not.


THE THREE THINGS WORTH YOUR TIME

**Your backups run, and a restore works.** Alice restored your May 11 database
onto a separate machine on May 13 and the data was all there. That is genuinely
good news and it is more than most companies your size can say.

**Restoring gives you your data but not your passwords.** The nightly dump does
not include the database's own user accounts. In a real recovery somebody would
restore everything successfully and then find the application unable to log in
to its own database, at the worst possible moment. We have now recorded those
credentials off the machine. The permanent fix is a one-line change to the
backup and I would like to make it.

**A full recovery takes about forty minutes.** That is the first real number
anybody has for how long Summit is offline in the worst case. It may be better
than that on your own hardware. I mention it because "we have backups" and "we
are back in forty minutes" are different promises and you should be making the
second one to your venues, not the first.


SOMETHING WE GOT WRONG IN APRIL

In Alice's first review she described WEB2 as doing four unrelated jobs and
suggested it was partly spare capacity. Having now looked properly, that is not
right.

WEB2 serves your images and stylesheets, and the application on WEB1 links to
them directly. WEB2 is not a spare. If it goes down during an event, WEB1 keeps
serving pages and they come out without images.

It also has no RAID, and it holds the only off-machine copy of your backups.

We are not proposing to change any of that this month, and none of it is
urgent. But you asked in April what WEB2 was for, and the honest answer is
different from the one we gave you.
