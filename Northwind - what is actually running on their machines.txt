WHY I LOOKED

Priya mentioned in passing that she had fixed the log cleanup on hr-www "a
while ago".  I asked which version was the good one and neither of us could
answer.

So I went and looked at all of them.  It took two afternoons and I wish I had
done it a year ago.


WHAT IS THERE

Scripts we have put on customer machines, not counting anything the customer or
their developer wrote:

    nw-cleanup      log and temp cleanup            on 7 of 10 machines
    nw-backup       database dump and rotate        on 4
    nw-diskreport   weekly mail of df output        on 3
    (unnamed)       various one-off fixes           at least 5

mon1's repository has none of these in it.  I versioned /usr/local/nwcheck last
June and stopped there, because at the time the thing that kept getting lost was
nwcheck.conf.  Everything we put on a customer machine has been copied there by
hand ever since and lives nowhere else.


nw-cleanup, WHICH IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS

Seven copies.  Four distinct versions.

    web1.summit     the original, March 2011
    web2.summit     the original
    ccweb1          the original plus a line Ruth asked for in 2011
    ccdata1         the original plus the same line
    ccdata2         the original plus the same line
    hr-www          PRIYA'S FIXED VERSION, March 2012
    bw-web1         the original

Priya's fix is real and the bug is real.  The original matches rotated logs
with a glob that also matches the live file, so on a machine where the log is
named without a suffix -- which is most of them -- it can delete the log Apache
is currently writing to.  Apache keeps writing to a deleted file until it is
restarted, so the symptom is that logs silently stop appearing and nothing
else happens.

She found it on hr-www in March because she went looking for a log that was not
there.  She fixed it on hr-www.  She did not know it was on six other machines,
because nothing says where we have put things.

I checked.  It has fired on web1.summit twice, in November 2011 and April 2012,
and both times we lost a day of access logs and did not notice.  There is
nothing else wrong on those machines.  We simply have two days of gaps in
Summit's access logs and I found out by reading a cleanup script.


WHAT ELSE THE CENSUS FOUND

    nw-diskreport mails to alice@ on two machines and to my old IBM address on

    One of the unnamed one-offs on bw-db1 is a mysqldump that runs at 03:00 and

    is not the customer's job either.  Left alone, noted, asked Dale.


WHAT WE ARE DOING ABOUT IT

The scripts go into mon1's repository, in customer-scripts/, one directory per
customer, and we push from there.  We stop editing them in place.

    nwpush <customer>        copy this customer's scripts to their machines
    nwpush -n <customer>     show what would change and do nothing

That is the whole of it.  It is scp in a loop with a diff first, and the diff
first is the part that matters -- I want to see what is on the machine before I
overwrite it, because today I have no idea.

I am not importing history that does not exist.  The first commit of these
files says March 2011 in the message and July 2012 in the date, because that is
the truth: they were written in March 2011 and this is the day they entered the
repository.

I am not writing a configuration manager.  We have ten machines and three
people and the thing that went wrong was not knowing where a file was.


WHAT I AM NOT FIXING TODAY

Whether a machine has drifted from what we pushed.  nwpush -n tells you when
you ask.  Nothing tells you when you do not ask.

That is the same shape as the problem I found last July on Summit's web2 and
the same shape as the one Priya found in March, and I am aware that I keep
writing that sentence.
