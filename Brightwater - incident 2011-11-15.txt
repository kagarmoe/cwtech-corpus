Brightwater Supply -- incident, Tuesday 15 November 2011

Prepared by:  Priya Raghunathan
Date:         16 November 2011
Status:       Internal

Dale telephoned at 09:14.  What he said, in full:

    "Catalogue's down, we can't take orders."

That is not a complaint about Dale.  It is the whole of what he knows and it is
accurate.


WHAT I CAN ESTABLISH

    09:14   Dale telephones.
    09:16   I load the catalogue.  It loads.  Product pages load.  Adding to
            a basket works.  Checkout returns a blank page.
    09:19   No alerts.  Both machines green.  bw-web1 and bw-db1 up, disk
            fine, mysql answering, http check passing -- it checks /catalog,
            which is fine, and not /checkout, which is not.
    09:31   Apache error log on bw-web1: PHP fatal error in the checkout
            module, calling a function that does not exist.
    09:48   The file containing that call has an mtime of 02:10 that morning.
    10:05   Dale confirms the vendor told him last week they would be
            "pushing an update overnight sometime this month".  He did not
            tell us.  He did not know he should.
    10:20   I telephone the vendor.  They will not talk to me.  I am not
            their customer.
    10:52   Dale telephones the vendor.
    13:30   Vendor pushes a fix.
    13:41   Checkout works.

Four and a half hours.  Roughly three of them mine.


WHOSE PROBLEM WAS IT

The vendor's.  It was their code, their update, and their fix.

Northwind spent three hours establishing that.  We could not have skipped the
three hours, because at 09:14 nobody on earth knew it was the vendor, and
finding out is the thing we sell.

That is the arrangement working exactly as John described it in April 2010 and
I do not think anybody should change it.

But we did three hours of unpaid work on somebody else's software, we cannot
bill the vendor because we have no relationship with them, and the monthly fee
is for running two Linux servers.


WHAT I WOULD HAVE WANTED

Not a different boundary.  The same boundary with a bill attached to the far
side of it.

Also: the http check watches /catalog because that is where I pointed it in
November 2010.  It should watch the thing the customer is trying to do.  I have
said this before about Summit -- it is in the alerts file under http, in my own
handwriting, since May.  I wrote it down and then did not do it.


WHAT I DID AFTERWARD

Added a checkout check on bw-web1 that fetches the checkout page and looks for
the submit button rather than the word "Brightwater".  It would have alerted at
02:14 instead of Dale at 09:14.

Did not add one at Summit.  Same problem, and the same reason as May: I have no
safe way to exercise a real purchase against production and Mark has still not
answered about a test event.  Asked him again.
