NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
What would have to be true about Fourmile

Prepared by:  Alice Chen
Date:         10 December 2018
Status:       Internal
Read by:      John, Marcus, Priya, Wes, Toby


I wrote a document like this in January 2014 before we signed Suite C, and I
have reread it, and the thing I got right was the format and not all of the
answers.

The format is: say what would have to be true, and say how we would find out we
were wrong.  If I cannot write the second part the first part is a hope.

Six things.


1.  THAT WE CAN SELL WHAT WE HAVE GOT

We hold Suite C to 31 March 2020.  Fourmile want three years and would take
five.

    We cannot sell a term we do not hold.  That is the rule we have applied to
    customers since the cage in 2013 and to hardware in 2016, and this is the
    first time we have had to apply it to ourselves, and we are failing it.

I want that sentence read slowly, because it is not a lease problem.  For four
years we have been careful to match every commitment to the commitment behind
it, and the whole time the commitment underneath all of them -- the room -- was
in a folder that the man who sells for us could not find.

    HOW WE FIND OUT WE ARE WRONG: we are already wrong.  This is not a risk,
    it is a finding.  What is open is what we do about it.

2.  THAT THE OPTION IS EXERCISABLE

Five years from April 2020, at market rate, notice by 30 September 2019.

We do not know the price.  Marcus has deliberately not asked, and he is right
not to have: the moment we ask, Renata knows we need it.

    So the honest shape of any Fourmile deal is that we are selling them a term
    that depends on an option we have not priced, and we have nine months to
    decide whether to exercise it.

    HOW WE FIND OUT WE ARE WRONG: we ask in the spring, and the market rate has
    moved against us, and we have already promised Fourmile five years.

3.  THAT EIGHTY-TWO THOUSAND IS RECOVERABLE

Marcus prices the work to 200 kW at $82,200.  About thirty of that is PDUs,
which travel.  Fifty stays in Alder Street's building.

Fourmile at seventy kilowatts is materially more than any customer we have had
except Halvard.  On any term of three years or more the work pays for itself
comfortably.  On sixteen months it does not.

    So the money follows the term and not the customer, which is the same thing
    I wrote about the suite in January 2014 and did not expect to be writing
    again.

    HOW WE FIND OUT WE ARE WRONG: we spend it, Fourmile give notice at
    twenty-four months, and we are holding fifty thousand of somebody else's
    electrical work and a room we have not renewed.

4.  THAT MARCUS CAN DO THIS AND EVERYTHING ELSE

Thirteen racks, live, at night, one at a time, over six to eight weeks, in a
room that also has to keep running.  Plus Fourmile's fourteen machines coming
in and more arriving during the term.  Plus the rota.  Plus whatever else
happens between now and February.

Toby can do the hands and does them better than Marcus.  Toby cannot negotiate
with Renata, cannot judge whether a rack fed for twelve will take twenty, and
cannot price an option.

    Marcus has written that sentence in one form or another in February 2014,
    September 2014, November 2014, June 2017 and August 2018.  I have written
    the reply each time and the reply has been that I know.

    HOW WE FIND OUT WE ARE WRONG: it is February, the electrician is in the
    room, Fourmile's machines are on a lorry, and Marcus is ill.

5.  THAT WE UNDERSTAND WHAT WE ARE AGREEING TO

They want hands at night, replacement, monitoring of power and temperature, and
a connection.  They do not want us near the work.

We have done all of that before, for Dev and for Dr Ma, at a fifth of the size.

    What we have not done before is put a future capacity number in a contract.
    Ellen wants to know what more she can have, when, and at what price, in
    writing, for years.  We have never sold that and I am not sure we should
    learn to on the largest deal we have been offered.

    HOW WE FIND OUT WE ARE WRONG: we write a schedule into a contract and then
    a thing we did not think of -- the facility, the electrician, an existing
    customer growing -- makes us unable to deliver it, and it is a breach and
    not an apology.

6.  THAT WE ARE NOT DOING THIS BECAUSE IT IS EXCITING

John wrote that this is the most money anybody has offered us in one
conversation, and then wrote that he is the wrong person to judge it.  He is
right on both counts and I am grateful for the second one.

    HOW WE FIND OUT WE ARE WRONG: we would have said yes to this at fifty
    kilowatts on a rolling arrangement, and we would not have had to think about
    any of the above, and it would have been a smaller and duller and better
    piece of business.


WHAT I AM PROPOSING

Not a yes and not a no.  Three things in an order.

    FIRST.  We go back to Fourmile with what is true.  Fifty kilowatts from
    February, on the racks we have, at a price John can write down.  A first
    term to 31 March 2020, which is sixteen months, and I will not pretend it is
    a number chosen for them.

    We tell them why.  Ellen has been let down by a supplier who promised what
    he could not deliver, and the way not to be that supplier is to say the
    awkward thing in the first meeting rather than the third.

    SECOND.  We ask Renata what the option costs.  Not in the spring.  Now,
    before we owe Fourmile anything, so that if the answer is bad we have lost
    a negotiating position and not a customer.

    Marcus is right that asking tells her something.  She is going to find out
    anyway the moment we order thirteen racks of circuits.

    THIRD.  We do not spend the eighty-two thousand until we know the answer to
    the second thing.

If the option is affordable we go back to Fourmile with a real term and the
seventy kilowatts they asked for, and we spend the money against a signed
contract, which is the rule and has been since 2013.

If it is not, we have sold them sixteen months of fifty kilowatts honestly, and
we have a year to decide what Northwind does about a room it cannot afford to
keep -- which is a question I would rather answer without a customer's machines
in it.


WHAT I AM NOT PROPOSING

Another building.  Buying anything.  Borrowing anything.  A second suite.
Hiring.

    I looked at all of them for a week.  Every one of them is a bigger decision
    than the one in front of us, and the reason to take a bigger decision is
    never that the smaller one is uncomfortable.

    -- AC
