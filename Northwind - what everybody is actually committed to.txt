NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
What everybody is actually committed to

Prepared by:  Priya Raghunathan
Date:         11 February 2019
Status:       Internal


Alice asked me what is behind the floor.

The question is simple.  Alder Street want five years and 180 kilowatts a month
whether we sell it or not.  What have our customers committed to, and until
when?

It took me nine days and I could not answer it from the records, which are
mine.


WHAT I FOUND

    Summit          $1,200/mo managed          30 days notice either way
    Clear Creek     $1,450/mo managed          30 days notice either way
    Brightwater     $1,600/mo managed          30 days notice either way
    Hartwell        $650/mo managed            30 days notice either way

    Halvard         94 kW in Suite C           see below
    Crestone        23 kW in Suite C           see below
    Sightline       11 kW, Dev's machines      see below
    Genomics core   13 kW, our machines        see below

The managed four I knew.  Thirty days either way, which is what we have sold
since 2010, and which means that against a five-year commitment they are worth
one month.

The other four are the room, and the room is the floor, and here is where I
had to go outside the records.

HALVARD.  Renewed 1 March 2017 for three years.  **Expires 29 February 2020.**

    One month before the lease we are being asked to sign starts.

    This is not in any record.  It is in a contract folder in the cabinet,
    which is where the Alder Street renewal was, and I found it the same way
    Marcus found that one -- by going and looking because somebody asked.

    Ninety-four of our hundred and forty-seven kilowatts.

CRESTONE.  No current term.

    They stopped paying in January 2015, we settled in the summer at a reduced
    commitment, and since then they have paid monthly against nothing.  Twenty-
    three kilowatts on no notice at all.

    I had to ask John what the settlement said because I could not find it.  He
    thinks thirty days.  He is not certain.

SIGHTLINE.  Dev's twelve-machine autumn commitment, 2016, no end date written
anywhere I can find.  He has paid every month for thirty-one months and I do not
believe he thinks he is under a term.

GENOMICS CORE.  Dr Ma's grant funded to September 2018.  **It ended five months
ago.**

    She has continued to pay and nobody has asked her what happens next.  We own
    fourteen nodes, thirteen kilowatts, bought against a commitment that has
    expired.  I did not know that until Tuesday.


SO: WHAT IS BEHIND THE FLOOR

    Committed beyond 1 April 2020, in writing, today          0 kW

Not a small number.  Zero.

Halvard expires a month before it starts.  Crestone are monthly.  Sightline is
undated.  Dr Ma is past the end of what funded her.  The managed four are thirty
days.

We are being asked to commit a hundred and eighty kilowatts a month for five
years against nothing that runs past March 2020.

    I want to be careful not to make that sound worse than it is.  Every one of
    those customers is paying, none of them has given notice, and most of them
    would probably renew.  "Probably" is the word doing the work and it is the
    word Alice has spent nine years taking out of documents.


WHAT I AM DOING ABOUT THE RECORDS

Every customer record gets a TERM heading.  What they are committed to, until
when, what notice, and where the paper is.

Same as CHANGES in March 2018 and for the same reason: I was holding something
in my head and in a filing cabinet that other people needed in order to do their
jobs, and when John needed it in November he could not get it.

    Where the paper is, specifically.  Twice in four months somebody has gone
    to the cabinet to find a term and the second time it was me.  A record that
    says "three years, expires 29 February 2020, contract in the 2017 folder"
    is worth more than one that says the first half.

That is an afternoon and it should have been an afternoon in 2014.


WHAT I AM NOT DOING

A contracts database.  A renewal calendar.  A system.

I have written four documents in nine years explaining why the records are flat
files and I am not going to argue with myself.  The TERM line has a date in it.
Reading the dates once a month is my job and it takes ten minutes.


THE THING I KEEP FINDING

This is the third time since November that somebody has gone looking for
something only one person knew, and found it in a cabinet.

    The lease, which Marcus had negotiated and not written down.
    What the room contains, which Marcus had measured and not summed.
    Now four customer terms, which are mine.

I do not think we have a filing problem.  I think we have six people who each
know something the company depends on, and a habit of writing down what we
decide and not what we are holding.
