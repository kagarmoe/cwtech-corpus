NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
Hartwell & Reed -- the invoice argument

Prepared by:  John Mercer
Date:         23 April 2012
Status:       Internal


WHAT HAPPENED

17 April, Cheryl telephones: the web site is showing an error and the partners
have noticed.

Priya spends four hours.  The CMS has stopped rendering the project pages after
a plugin update that Cheryl authorised in March through the firm that does
their marketing.  It is the CMS, it is not the server, and the server never
went down.

We invoiced three hours at $95 on the 20th, correctly, with notice first, per
the January terms.

Cheryl replied on the 23rd.  I am quoting it because paraphrasing it would let
me off:

    "I am confused and I would rather ask than assume.  We pay Northwind
    every month to look after the website.  The website was broken and
    Northwind fixed it.  I do not understand what the extra invoice is for.
    Please do not think I am refusing to pay it.  I would just like to be
    able to explain it to Mr Reed."


SHE IS NOT WRONG

We do not look after the website.  We look after the machine the website is on.
That distinction is in her record, it is in the agreement she signed, and Alice
put the CMS on the known-problems list in January 2011 and we told them in
writing that nobody was maintaining it.

None of that is the same as Cheryl understanding it.

What she has is a conversation with me in January 2011, in which I said we
would look after their server, and fifteen months of us doing exactly that
without her ever needing to know where the line was.  The line only becomes
visible when it costs her money.

I predicted this in January.  I wrote that when the Hartwell CMS broke, "this
is the clause that decides who pays for the afternoon."  I was pleased with the
sentence.  I did not think about who would be reading the invoice.


THE GENERAL PROBLEM

Alice and Priya have both said a version of this and I have twice said no.

Four customers -- six now -- and every one of them has a different
understanding of what they are buying, because every one of them got a
different conversation.

    Laura knows exactly where the line is.  She has been on the wrong side of
    an outage and she reads what Alice sends her.

    Ruth knows better than we do and tells us when we are wrong.

    Dale believes nothing before seven in the morning is urgent.  That is his
    view and not the agreement, and it is written in his record as his view,
    and if something breaks at five he is going to be surprised.

    Cheryl believed we look after her website.

    Larkspur Vineyards and Cortez Brothers Paving both signed in March on
    language I wrote fresh each time because I was in a hurry.

That last one is the part that is my fault and not anybody's misunderstanding.


WHAT WE ARE DOING

Not a service catalogue.  I have said no to that twice and I still think a
company our size answering the telephone with "it depends what you bought" is
worse than the problem.

One page per customer, in their words, saying what we do and what we do not,
sent to them, signed off by them.  Not a contract -- the agreements stand.  A
page they can put in a drawer and hand to the person who asks.

Alice's summary when I finally agreed: "So you have said yes to writing it down
for the customer and no to writing it down for us, which is an interesting
order to do it in."

She is right and I am doing it in that order anyway, because the customer
misunderstanding is the one costing us money this month.

We are not billing Hartwell for the three hours.  Not because the invoice was
wrong.  Because Cheryl asked a fair question and the answer is that we never
gave her a way to know.
