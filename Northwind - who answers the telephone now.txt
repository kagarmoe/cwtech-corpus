NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
Who answers the telephone now

Prepared by:  Alice Chen
Date:         12 November 2012
Status:       Internal


THE CHANGE

From 1 December the out-of-hours number stops ringing my mobile.

It rings whoever is on the rota that week.  Priya, Marcus, or me.  Wes is not on
it yet and will not be until he has sat with each of us through a real one.

I have been the number since May 2010.  John and I both wrote at the time that
it was not a rota and it was not a plan, and that it would be the first thing
to break if we took a second customer.  We took five more.  It did not break so
much as quietly cost me every evening for two and a half years, which is a
slower and less obvious way for a thing to fail.


WHAT I AM KEEPING

Architecture.  What we build and how.

Escalation.  If the person on the rota does not know, they telephone me, at any
hour, and I would rather be woken for something small than not woken.

Laura, Ruth and Dale.  Not because the others cannot handle them -- Priya has
handled Dale better than I do for a year -- but because those three took a
chance on a two-person company and they should be able to reach the person who
looked at their systems in the first place.


WHAT I AM NOT KEEPING

First response.  Onboarding.  Writing every script.  Being the only person who
knows where anything is.

That last one has been the actual problem since 2011 and everything we have
written down since has been an attempt at it.


WHO DOES WHAT NOW

This is not an organisation chart.  We are five people and I do not want one.
It is a list of who to ask.

    Priya       Operations.  The rota, the alerts file, the checks, whether we
                are meeting what we promised.  She has been doing this since
                she arrived and I am writing it down rather than changing it.

    Marcus      Facilities and hardware.  Providers, cross-connects, power,
                anything that involves a person standing in a cold aisle.  He
                has done that job from the other side, and before that in the
                Navy where there was no other side to telephone.

    Wes         Routine administration.  The queue of small things.  Not on the
                rota yet.

    Alice       Architecture, escalation, the three customers above.

    John        Customers, contracts, money, and now hiring, which he has
                discovered he is worse at than he expected.


WHAT MARCUS FOUND IN HIS FIRST MONTH

Worth recording separately because it is the thing I would not have found.

We have no hardware relationship with three of our six providers.  We have
names of account managers for two of them, both of which are people who have
left.  Clear Creek's ccweb1 sits at Cheyenne Mountain, where we have no account
at all, and the plan if its hardware fails is to telephone Ruth and ask her to
telephone them.  I wrote that down in January 2011 and wrote "still never
tested" and it is still true and it is now two years old.

Marcus's view, which I am quoting because it is shorter than mine: "You have
been lucky.  Not wrong.  Lucky."

He is fixing it.  That is what he is for.
