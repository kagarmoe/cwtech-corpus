CAIRNWELL TECHNOLOGIES
Summit Ticketing -- the New Year on-sale, 7 December 2012

Prepared by:  Priya Raghunathan
Date:         10 December 2012
Status:       Internal


WHAT HAPPENED

Summit put four New Year's Eve events on sale at 10:00 on Friday the 7th.

Nothing went down.  Every machine stayed up, every check stayed green, and the
site kept serving.  Purchases took between thirty and ninety seconds to
complete, for about two and a half hours.

Laura says roughly a third of the people who started a purchase did not finish
one.  She has that from her own reports, not from us.  We have no way to know
how many people gave up, which is the second thing I want to talk about.


WHY IT WAS SLOW

The orders table on web1.summit has 4.1 million rows.  In July 2010 it had 1.9
million.

It is MyISAM.  Every insert takes a lock on the whole table.  Under an ordinary
event that is invisible.  Under four simultaneous on-sales it is not: inserts
queue, the queue makes each purchase slower, slower purchases mean more of them
overlapping, and the thing feeds itself until demand drops.

It did not fall over because it never quite ran out of connections.  It got to
71 of 100 and stayed there.  Which is why nothing alerted -- mysql_threads
warns at 60 and criticals at 85.  It warned.  It warned for two and a half
hours.

Alice wrote in May 2010 that MySQL on that box is configured for a machine with
less memory than it has, and that it would be the first thing she looked at if
anyone asked why the site was slow under load.  Somebody has now asked.


THE PART THAT IS OUR FAULT

There is no index on orders.email.

We found that in July 2010, during the first incident.  We decided not to add it
because adding an index to a large MyISAM table locks it, and it was July, and
Summit was selling.  We wrote "after the season".

That was two and a half years ago.  There have been four seasons since.  Nobody
was wrong on any single day -- every individual decision to wait was reasonable
-- and the thing still did not get done, because "after the season" is not a date
and nobody owned it.

I went back through the mail archive.  mysql_threads has fired a warning on
web1.summit eleven times since it was added in July 2010.  Every one of them
self-resolved within the hour and every one of them was ignored, including four
by me.  The eleven are in chronological order and they are not evenly spaced.
They cluster, and the clusters are getting closer together.

We had the data.  It arrived in our mail, one message at a time, for two years,
and there was no point at which anyone looked at all of it at once.


WHAT WE DID

    7 Dec, 12:40   Demand dropped on its own.  Nothing we did fixed it.
    8 Dec          Alice and I agreed we are not touching a production table
                   during Summit's December.
    17 Dec         Told Laura what it was, what we should have done in 2010,
                   and when we are going to do it.
    14 Jan         Index added, 03:00, agreed maintenance window, 41 seconds
                   of table lock.  First maintenance window we have ever
                   agreed with a customer.

Three years and we had never once arranged to do a thing at a time that suited
the customer.  We have always either done it immediately or not done it.


WHAT I TOLD LAURA

That we found this in 2010, wrote it down, decided to wait, and then did not
have a way of coming back to it.

She asked whether there were other things like that.  I said I did not know and
that I would find out, which was the only honest answer and not a comfortable
one.

There are eleven.  See the deferred list.
