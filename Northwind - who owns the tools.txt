NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
Who owns the tools

Prepared by:  Alice Chen
Reviewed by:  Priya Raghunathan, Wes Okafor
Date:         19 June 2017
Status:       Internal


WHAT MADE ME WRITE THIS

mon1 filled up in February and nothing was monitored for three hours.

We fixed it in a morning and the fix was correct and small.  What has bothered me
since is the answer to a question nobody asked at the time, which is: whose job
was it to notice that a file had been growing for four years?

It was nobody's.  It was everybody's, which is the same thing.

Priya wrote nwcheck in 2010 because we needed it.  She wrote the history file in
2012 because of Summit.  Alice wrote nwhosts in 2011.  Toby wrote the rotation
this February.  Marcus wrote the temperature checks in 2014.  Five people, seven
years, one directory, and not one of them was ever given the job.

That worked when there were three of us.  There are six of us and eight
customers and a suite, and the thing those five people built by accident is now
the only reason we know anything.


THE SPLIT

Two jobs that have been one job.

**Running the customers.**  The rota, escalations, whether we are meeting what
we promised, onboarding, being the person a customer speaks to.

**Keeping the tools.**  nwcheck, the checks, nwhosts, nwpush, nwtrend, mon1
itself, and knowing when one of them is quietly becoming a problem.

Priya has done both since 2011.  She has done both well and she has been telling
me for a year, in the mild way she tells me things, that the second one only
gets done when the first one is quiet.


WHO DOES WHICH

Priya takes running the customers.  That is where she is best and it is what she
was hired to do, and she said so plainly when I asked rather than letting me
decide it for her.

**Wes takes the tools.**

He started in 2012 doing exactly what he was told and writing down every place
the instructions were wrong.  He is now the person who has read all of it most
recently and most carefully, which turns out to be the qualification.

    I asked him whether he wanted it.  He said he did, and then he said the
    thing that decided it for me: "I did not write any of it, so I will have to
    read it before I change it.  That is probably better."

Priya's condition, which is fair: he does not touch a check that is currently
catching something without telling her first.


WHAT WE ARE NOT DOING

**Not making teams.**  Six people do not have teams.  Wes still racks machines
when the suite is busy and Priya still writes a check when she needs one.  This
is about who is accountable when nobody is looking, not about who is allowed to
do what.

**Not separating the repository.**  The checks, the records, the customer
scripts and the customer pages all live in one place on mon1 because they all
lived on mon1.  Splitting them is a day of work and an ongoing tax and we have
no reason yet.

What we are doing instead is writing down who owns what path, in a file, so
that a question like February's has an answer.

**Not writing job titles.**  Nobody's card changes.  Marcus asked whether his
said anything and I had to tell him we have never printed cards.


THE THING I HAVE NOT SOLVED

Marcus is still one person and the only one who understands the facilities.  He
asked for a second in February 2014 and I recorded the delay as mine in October
of that year, and it is now 2017 and he is still the only one.

Toby can do the hands.  Toby cannot negotiate a power contract or tell me
whether a room will take another forty kilowatts.

I am not going to pretend this document addresses that.  It does not.  It is on
the deferred list with my name on it and a date, which is better than it was and
is not the same as fixed.
