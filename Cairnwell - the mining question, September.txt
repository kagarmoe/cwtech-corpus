CAIRNWELL TECHNOLOGIES
The mining question

Prepared by:  John Mercer
Date:         16 September 2013
Status:       Internal


WHERE THIS HAS GOT TO

Since June I have had nine enquiries about hosting mining hardware.  Two were
people Gary knows.  The rest found us some other way, and one of them said we
were recommended on a forum, which is not a sentence anyone has ever said to me
about Cairnwell before.

Of the nine:

    four    small, four to fifteen machines, Lindqvist-shaped
    three   serious enough to want terms and dates
    one     did not know what he wanted
    one     Halvard Mining

Halvard is the one that changes the question.  Petra Ganz runs it.  She spent
fifteen years running IT for a grocery chain and left last year, and she sends
an agenda before a telephone call.  She asked me about contract term, remedies
and what happens on default before she asked me anything technical.

She wants somewhere in the region of a hundred kilowatts.

That is ten Lindqvists.  It is about sixteen times everything else Cairnwell
manages put together.


WHAT I WANT TO DO

Stop answering these one at a time.

Every one of these conversations ends the same way: Marcus works out what it
would take, we find out we cannot do it inside our current arrangements, and
either the number is small enough to fit in a cabinet or it is not.  That is a
fortnight of our time per enquiry to say no to most of them.

If we had space — our own cage, our own power feed, room to put people in — we
could say yes in an afternoon and we would have said yes to three of these nine.

I am not asking to bet the company.  I am asking whether the thing Alice said in
March is available: a room that would still be worth having if every one of
these machines went in a skip.

Because if it is available, the argument against is only that we might not fill
it, and we have nine enquiries in three months.


WHAT I KNOW I DO NOT KNOW

Bitcoin was about ninety dollars in July and it is about a hundred and thirty
now.  Every one of these people is more confident than they were in the spring
and I cannot tell how much of that is the price.

The hardware is changing.  All four of the serious enquiries mentioned the new
machines that do only mining.  Two of them are waiting on orders they placed
months ago and have not received.  Gary says the whole thing is full of people
taking money for hardware that does not exist yet.

I do not know what that means for us.  It might mean nothing — a rack is a rack
and a kilowatt is a kilowatt, and if their machines change we are still selling
the same room.  It might mean the people who are paying us now cannot pay us in
a year.

Alice will have views and I would rather have them in writing before I talk to
Petra again.
