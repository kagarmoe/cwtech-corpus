HOW THIS HAPPENED

In March we told Gary no.  The reasons were the load, the facility and the
building argument, and none of them have changed.

What changed is the size.  His landlord gave him notice in April.  He has sold
fourteen rigs, kept seven in Boulder where the supply can carry them, and asked
whether we could take ten.

Ten rigs is about 9 kW with cooling.  That is a high-density cabinet, not a
room.  Alder Street will do one at 10 kW, metered, with a demand charge I have
now seen in writing.  I got that price in April doing the work John asked for
in March, which is the only reason we could answer him in two days instead of a
fortnight.

So the answer this time is yes, and it is yes for the same reasons March was
no.  The arithmetic moved, not the principle.


WHAT IS DIFFERENT ABOUT THESE MACHINES

I have written this out because everyone here has spent three years learning
one set of instincts and most of them are wrong for this cabinet.

    They are never idle.  Every other machine we manage has a night.  A web
    server at four in the morning is doing nothing and the cooling has a rest.
    These draw the same at four in the morning as at four in the afternoon,
    forever.  Design for the peak and then remember there is no trough.

    Heat is the whole job.  On a normal cabinet the thermal problem is a
    consequence of the computing.  Here it is the product.  Every watt that
    goes in comes out as heat six inches away.

    Downtime is arithmetic, not embarrassment.  When Summit is down, Laura is
    upset and we do not know what it cost her.  When this cabinet is down,
    Gary knows what it cost to the cent before we have finished apologising.
    He will not be angrier than Laura.  He will be more specific.

    A dead machine is not an incident.  A GPU dies, he unplugs it and plugs in
    another.  There is nothing on it.  No backup, no restore, no data, no
    migration.  Three years of everything we know about hardware failure is
    irrelevant here, and the temptation is to apply it anyway.

    Nothing is production in the sense we mean.  There is no user waiting.  If
    a machine is off for an hour it has lost an hour of arithmetic, and that
    is all that has happened.


WHAT WE AGREED TO DO

    The cabinet, its power and its cooling.
    Telling him when a machine stops.
    Remote hands -- reseating, swapping a card he has couriered over.
    Talking to Alder Street, because he is not their customer and we are.

WHAT WE DID NOT AGREE TO DO

    Anything about the mining software.  He runs it, he configures it, he
    decides what it points at.  We do not touch it and we do not advise on it.

    Anything about whether it is worth doing.  Not our business, and if it
    stops being worth doing that is between him and the arithmetic.

    Buying hardware.  His machines, his money, his choice.  We rack what he
    sends.


WHAT WE ARE WATCHING, AND WHY IT IS NOT WHAT WE USUALLY WATCH

The cabinet has a metered PDU.  Alder Street gives us a reading.  Total draw
tells us more than any single machine does: if it falls by roughly a tenth,
one has stopped, and we do not need to know which to telephone him.

Priya has added two checks.  One reads the cabinet draw and alerts on a step
change.  One asks each rig what it is doing, over ssh, because they run Linux
like anything else.

We are not monitoring hash rate as a performance measure.  We are using it to
tell whether a machine is alive, which is the only thing we have agreed to
tell him.

    That distinction matters and I want it written down before somebody blurs
    it.  The moment we report on how well he is mining, we have opinions about
    his business, and then we own something we did not agree to own.  We have
    been here before with Mark and with a catalogue vendor.


ONE THING I AM UNEASY ABOUT

We are one cabinet into this and it is nine kilowatts of a ten kilowatt
allowance.  There is no room to grow inside this arrangement, and if Gary asks
for five more machines the answer is another cabinet or another building.

I would rather say that now than discover it when he asks.
