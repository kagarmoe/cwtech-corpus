NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
What happened on 17 February and what I could not do about it

Prepared by:  Marcus Reyes
Date:         19 February 2018
Status:       Internal


I do not usually write these.  Priya asked for it and she is right to.


TIMELINE

    Fri 16 Feb, 18:40   Racked gc-node09 through gc-node14 in Suite C.  Six
                        nodes, Dr Ma's second grant.  Power, cross-connects,
                        burn-in started.  Photographs in the suite folder.

    Fri 16 Feb, 21:15   Added the six to nwcheck.conf, one rig_alive line
                        each, because a machine that is racked and not
                        watched is a machine nobody finds out about.  This
                        is what we have always done and I would do it again.

    Sat 17 Feb, 02:40   Six PROBLEM mails.  The out-of-hours number rang.

    Sat 17 Feb, 02:52   Read the mails.  Every one of them says:

                            check said OK and wrote to stderr: Warning:
                            Permanently added 'gc-node09' (ECDSA) to the
                            list of known hosts.

    Sat 17 Feb, 02:55   Logged in, looked at all six.  Every node up, every
                        node mining nothing and rendering nothing because
                        they are in burn-in, every check returning what it
                        should return.  Nothing is wrong with the hardware.

    Sat 17 Feb, 03:10   Established what it is.  ssh says that line the
                        first time it sees a host.  It says it on stderr.
                        Since November, nwcheck treats a check that exits
                        zero and writes to stderr as a failure.

                        The check was right.  The node is fine.  ssh made a
                        remark and we turned it into a critical.

    Sat 17 Feb, 03:20   Here is the part I want on the record.

                        I knew what it was inside thirty minutes and there
                        was nothing I was able to do about it.

                        The fix is nine lines in nwcheck.  nwcheck is Wes's.
                        It was a quarter past three on a Saturday.  I was
                        not going to telephone him to have him edit a file
                        so I could go back to bed, and I was not going to
                        edit it myself at that hour in a language I do not
                        write, on the machine that watches everything else.

    Sat 17 Feb, 03:25   So I did the only thing available to me, which was
                        the wrong one.  Commented the six lines out of
                        nwcheck.conf.  The mails stopped.

    Sat 17 Feb, 03:30   Wrote the note below and went to bed.

    Mon 19 Feb, 08:05   Told Wes.  He had them running again by ten, with
                        the fix, and the six nodes were watched from then.

    Mon 19 Feb, 08:30   Told Priya, who found out from me on Monday about
                        a customer of hers being unmonitored since Saturday,
                        which is not how she should find that out.


THE NOTE I LEFT

    gc-node09..14 commented out of nwcheck.conf 03:25 17 Feb.
    NOT because anything is wrong with them.
    Because ssh's first-contact warning now criticals and I cannot stop it.
    THESE SIX MACHINES ARE NOT WATCHED UNTIL SOMEBODY UNCOMMENTS THEM.
    -- MR


WHAT THIS COST

Six machines unmonitored from 03:25 on the Saturday until ten on the Monday.
Fifty-four and a half hours.  Nothing happened to them.  That is luck and I
have used that word here before.

Four people's Saturday morning, if you count Wes and Priya finding out about it
afterwards, which you should.

And the thing I did at 03:25 is the thing we wrote a document about in November.
We went twenty-two days blind because a check could not fail.  Three months
later the only tool the man on watch had was to switch six checks off.


WHAT I AM NOT SAYING

I am not saying the November change was wrong.  It caught a real class of thing
and I would keep it.  Wes ran it past everybody, ran his new nwtry, and pasted
the output in the commit, and none of that was ever going to find this, because
this needs a host ssh has not seen before and every host in his test had been
seen a thousand times.

I am not saying anybody should have foreseen it.  I racked six machines on a
Friday night.  That is the first time we have added six hosts at once since
Gary's rigs in 2013, and in 2013 nwcheck did not care about stderr.

I am saying that the person holding the telephone at three in the morning could
see the problem, could name the problem, and had no legal move except the one
that makes us blind.


WHAT I WOULD LIKE

Not a process.  I have been at two places that answered this with a process and
both of them are slower than us and neither of them is better.

Two things.

One: something the man on watch can edit.  If the fix is "ignore this exact
sentence from ssh", that should be a line in a file, with a date on it and a
reason, not nine lines in somebody's shell script.  I can write a line in a
file at three in the morning.  I should not be editing nwcheck at three in the
morning and I am not going to.

Two: whoever wrote the change should be on the telephone rota.  I have said
this before in a different context and I will keep saying it.  It is not a
punishment and it is not about fairness.  It is that you find out what you have
built when it wakes you up.

    Wes is the only person here who writes the things that wake the rest of us
    up and has never been woken by one.

    That is not his fault.  Alice took him off the list in 2012 because he was
    new.  He has not been new for six years and nobody moved him back.
