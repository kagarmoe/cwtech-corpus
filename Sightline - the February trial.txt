NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
Sightline -- the February trial

Prepared by:  Priya Raghunathan
Contributions from: Toby Whelan, Marcus Reyes
Date:         22 February 2016
Status:       Internal

Three months, four of Dev's machines, ended 19 February.  This is what we found
out, including the parts that do not flatter us.


WHAT WE DID

Four render nodes, his hardware, couriered in November.  Racked in Suite C
alongside Halvard.  He pushes jobs to them over a VPN from his office and pulls
the output back.

We agreed: the machines, the power, the cooling, remote hands, and telling him
when something has stopped.  Not the render software, not the jobs, not the
network at his end.

Paid: $1,100 a month, which Marcus costed and which is roughly break-even before
anybody's time.


WHAT WORKED

The building.  Never in question and never a problem.  Power, cooling and
containment did not notice four more machines, and the thermal swing Marcus was
worried about did not materialise at this scale.  It would at forty machines.

Remote hands.  Toby swapped a failed drive in eleven minutes on a Saturday in
January and Dev's job finished on time.  That is the single thing Dev has
mentioned to other people.

Our records.  Dev's four machines got a record, checks and an entry in the
deferred list like any customer, and it took Toby about an hour because we have
been doing this for six years.


WHAT DID NOT WORK

**We could not tell him when a render had failed.**

Our checks say the machine is up, drawing power, at temperature and reachable.
All four were green for eleven hours on 3 February while a job had died at
about 40 per cent because his software ran out of scratch space.

He found out in the morning.  He was decent about it and he was also quite
clear that this is the thing he is paying for.

    We have no honest way to check that from outside.  The failure was inside
    his application, on a disk we do not monitor, in software we agreed not to
    touch.  I could watch disk on those machines — that is trivial and I have
    now done it — but the next failure will be a different one.

    This is the same wall we hit at Summit in 2010 and never got past: nothing
    checks that a customer can complete a purchase.  Six years later I am
    writing the same sentence about renders.

**The data transfer is his whole experience of us.**

Four hundred gigabytes in, per job, over a VPN from an office on business
broadband.  Eleven hours, sometimes fourteen.  His renders take four.

Nothing about that is our fault and all of it is our problem, because from where
he sits the slow part is "Northwind".  Marcus says the fix is a connection into
the facility that costs more per month than Dev pays us.

**We were one person deep.**

Toby did every physical thing.  When he was away in December, Marcus did it, and
Marcus was at Alder Street four times that week for Halvard as well.  At four
machines this is fine.  At forty it is not, and we already knew that and wrote
it down in February 2014.


WHAT DEV SAID

He wants to continue.  He would take twelve machines rather than four for his
autumn, and he asked what it would cost for us to hold capacity he does not
always use, which is a question nobody has asked us before and which I do not
think we have an answer to.

He also said, and I am quoting because John will want it: "I do not want to run
a machine room.  I have tried and I am bad at it and it is not what I am for."


WHAT I THINK THIS MEANS

Not a service yet.  We have one customer, one shape of workload, one trial, and
three problems we have not solved.

What I would say is that none of the three problems is about the building, and
the building was the thing we were unsure about.

    The failure-visibility problem is a service-definition problem.  Either we
    get inside the workload, which we have refused to do for everybody since
    2010, or we tell customers plainly that we do not watch their jobs and mean
    it.

    The data problem is a money problem with a known price.

    The depth problem is a hiring problem we identified two years ago.

I am not recommending anything.  I said in October that I did not think the
hardware question was mine to answer and it still is not, and Dev has now asked
a harder version of it — what does it cost to reserve capacity nobody is using.
