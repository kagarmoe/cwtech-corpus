SUMMIT TICKETING -- WHAT I CAN SAY

John Mercer. Started 16 July 2010, after the night of the 15th.
One page. Keep it one page.

I spent twenty minutes on the telephone with Laura on Thursday and the only
true thing I could tell her was that Alice was looking at it. That is not
enough and it is not Alice's job to fix it while she is fixing the server.

This is not Alice's inventory. Hers is nine pages and organised by machine.
This is the six things I need in my hand when the phone rings.


WHO AND WHAT

Laura Bennett owns it. Mark Ellis wrote the software and is a contractor, not
her employee. Seven staff. Selling season May to September.


THE TWO MACHINES

WEB1 is the one that matters. The site and the database are both on it.
WEB2 does the images, the nightly reports, and holds a copy of the backups.
WEB2 is not a spare. If WEB2 goes, the site stays up without pictures.

Both are leased from Mile High. Mile High replaces hardware and reboots on
request. They do not touch the operating system or the software.


WHAT WE ARE RESPONSIBLE FOR

The machines, the operating system, Apache, the database staying up, disk,
backups, monitoring, and first response.

Not whether the software is correct. That is Mark.

When nobody knows which it is, it is ours until we know. Then it goes where it
belongs. That is the sentence. Use it.


WHAT I CAN PROMISE ON THE PHONE

That we are looking, and what we know so far, including when that is nothing.

Not when it will be fixed. Ever. At any hour.

Out of hours the number rings Alice. There is no second person. Laura knows
this and I told her myself.


WHAT TO ASK ALICE FOR, IN THIS ORDER

    Is it up or is it down?
    Can people buy tickets? (Not the same question. 15 July.)
    Is it ours or Mark's, or do we not know yet?
    What do you need from Mark or from Laura?
    When should I call her back?

That last one is the only commitment I should make without checking.


WHAT I MUST NOT DO

Guess at a cause on the telephone. On the 15th it would have been reasonable
to say "sounds like the database" and I would have been half right in a way
that turned out to matter, because the database was the symptom and not the
problem.

Say anything about March. We do not know what happened in March. Alice has
written this down twice now.


WHAT IS ON THE LIST AND NOT DONE

Alice's cheap fixes from May: the backup takes no lock, the backup leaves out
the database passwords, the application files are not backed up at all. WEB2
has no RAID and holds the only off-site copy. None of it is urgent. All of it
is on a list Laura has not seen because I decided not to open the first month
of a monthly agreement with an invoice.

If Laura ever asks me whether there is anything I have not told her, the answer
is yes and this is it.
