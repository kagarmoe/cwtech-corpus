CAIRNWELL TECHNOLOGIES
The second person for the room

Prepared by:  Marcus Reyes
Date:         21 November 2014
Status:       Internal


I asked for this in February.  Alice wrote in October that we had not done it
and that the delay was hers.  It is done.  Toby Whelan started on the 17th.

This is what I wrote before we advertised, so that the answer to "why him" is on
paper and not in my head.


WHAT THE JOB ACTUALLY IS

Not an engineer.  Not a second me.  I want to be exact about that because John
asked twice whether we were hiring somebody who could take over from me, and the
answer is no, and pretending otherwise would have got us the wrong person.

    Hands in the suite.  Racking, cabling, cross-connects, PDU work, swapping
    a drive or a PSU at two in the morning without being talked through it.

    Escorting.  Customers come into our suite now.  Somebody has to walk them
    in, watch what they do, and know when to say no.

    Reading the room.  Airflow, blanking panels, containment left open, a
    cabinet drawing more than its neighbours.  This is learnable and it is not
    obvious and it is the thing that keeps a dense room from cooking itself.

    Being there when I am not.  Since February there has been exactly one
    person who can stand in that room and know what he is looking at, and I
    have said in writing that it could not always be him.

That is a real job for one person and it is not half of mine.


WHAT I DID NOT ASK FOR

A degree.  Nobody in that room has ever needed one.

Scripting.  Priya and Wes cover that.  If he picks it up, good; if he does not,
the job still gets done.  I would rather have somebody who has been on a night
shift.

Somebody who wants my job.  We are six people.  There is nowhere for him to be
promoted to and I am not going to pretend there is.


WHO WE HIRED

Twenty-six.  Four years at a wholesale colocation provider in Colorado Springs
-- remote hands, cross-connects, escorting, hardware replacement.  Night shift
twice.  A CompTIA certificate his employer paid for.  No degree.

I counted, roughly, from what he described: something like ten thousand
cross-connects.  He has done more cabling than I have.

Two things decided it.

    He said he came because we were smaller and he thought he would be allowed
    to think.  Everybody says a version of that.  He then described a thing he
    had noticed at the last place and been told not to worry about, which was
    correct and which nobody had asked him for.

    I asked him what he would do if he found a cabinet at fourteen kilowatts.
    He said he would not touch it, he would find out who put it there and what
    the cabinet was rated for, and he would tell somebody before he did
    anything.  That is the right answer and it is not the answer somebody gives
    to sound careful.  It is what you say when you have been in a room where
    somebody did touch it.


WHAT THIS DOES NOT FIX

It does not fix the single point of failure and I do not want anybody reading
this in a year and thinking it did.

Toby can do the hands.  Toby cannot negotiate a power contract, cannot tell you
whether Alder Street will sell us another forty kilowatts or is bluffing, and
cannot look at a room and say what it will hold in two years.  That is still
one person and that person is me.

I asked in February for a second pair of hands, which is what I have got and
what I asked for.  The other thing is a different hire, it costs more than this
one, and I am not asking for it in the same month we hired.


WHAT HE IS INHERITING

Suite C, nine months old, drawing somewhere around a hundred and forty against
a floor of a hundred and eighty.  Four customers: Halvard, Crestone, Vail, and
Gary Lindqvist's three kilowatts in the corner, which he cut down to from nine
in August and which costs us nothing we were not already paying for.  A
containment arrangement that works and that nobody has written down.  The
temperature and draw checks I put in in February, which fire correctly and
which nobody but me has ever acted on.

He will be the second person who has ever seen that room properly.  I am going
to walk him round it every day for a fortnight, and then I am going to leave him
in it for a week while I am at Alder Street, which is the only way I will find
out whether this worked.
