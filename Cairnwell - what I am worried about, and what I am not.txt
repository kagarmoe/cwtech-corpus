CAIRNWELL TECHNOLOGIES
What I am worried about, and what I am not

Prepared by:  Alice Chen
Date:         21 October 2013
Status:       Internal

John asked for this in writing.  Marcus has read it and disagrees with one part,
which I have marked.


WHAT I AM NOT WORRIED ABOUT

The work.  A room with power and cooling is a solved problem and Marcus has
solved it before.  I am not nervous about our ability to run it.

The customers being strange.  They are less strange than Mark Ellis.  They want
one thing, they know what it costs them per hour, and they tell us when it is
broken.  Lindqvist has been the easiest four months we have had.

The hardware changing.  This is the one everybody keeps raising and I think it
is the weakest objection.  If their GPUs are replaced by machines that do only
mining, we are still selling power and floor and cooling.  The load might go up
per cabinet, which is a design problem and not an existential one.  A rack does
not care what is in it.

    Marcus disagrees.  His point: denser machines mean more kilowatts in the
    same floor, and a room designed for 8 kW a cabinet is not a room designed
    for 20.  If we build for what exists now and the machines double, we have
    built the wrong room.  I think he is right about the engineering and I do
    not think it changes the decision, but it should be in here in his words
    and not mine.


WHAT I AM WORRIED ABOUT

**One.  The power commitment outlives the customer commitment.**

This is the whole thing and everything else is smaller.

A lease with a power feed is three years, probably five.  Petra has not offered
us three years.  Nobody in this business has offered anybody three years.  These
are people whose own revenue depends on a price that moved from ninety dollars
to a hundred and thirty in eight weeks, and if it moves the other way they will
not be paying anybody.

If we sign five years of power against one year of customers, we have not taken
a business risk.  We have taken a bet, with a landlord as the counterparty.

**Two.  Concentration.**

If Halvard alone is a hundred kilowatts and our whole managed business is six,
then within a year most of Cairnwell's revenue is one customer in one industry.

Summit, Clear Creek, Brightwater and Hartwell do not fail at the same time as
each other.  Six mining customers do.  They all depend on the same number.

That is not a reason to refuse.  It is a reason to know what percentage we are
willing to be, before we are it, rather than discovering it afterwards.

**Three.  We have never been the landlord.**

Everything we have ever sold has been our attention.  A room is not our
attention.  If the room is half empty we pay for it anyway, and there is no
version of working harder that fixes an empty cage.

I am not saying that is bad.  I am saying it is a different company and we
should say so out loud rather than arriving there.


WHAT I ACTUALLY THINK WE SHOULD DO

I said in March that I would do this with a room that is still worth having if
the machines go in a skip.  I meant it and I still mean it.

So: take the space if two things are true.

    The power commitment does not exceed the customer commitment behind it.
    If Petra will sign two years, we will take two years.  If she will sign
    one, we take one, or we take a smaller room.

    The room is somewhere we would put ordinary customers.  Not a shed on a
    ranch with a substation.  A facility we would be willing to move Summit
    into, if it came to that.

If both are true I am for it, and I will say so to John in those words.

If the only room available is one we can only fill with mining, then we are not
taking a room, we are entering a different business, and that decision deserves
more than a fortnight and a good quarter.


WHAT I AM NOT DOING

Predicting.  I do not know whether this lasts, and I have read enough of what
the people in it write to be confident that they do not either.

I am also not going to pretend the cautious answer is the wise one.  If this
runs for five years and we sat it out, that will also have been a decision, and
nobody will write it down because nothing will have happened.
