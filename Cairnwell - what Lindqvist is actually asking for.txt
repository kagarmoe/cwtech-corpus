CAIRNWELL TECHNOLOGIES
What Lindqvist is actually asking for

Prepared by:  Marcus Reyes
Date:         4 March 2013
Status:       Internal

John asked what it would take.  Here is what it would take.


THE NUMBER THAT MATTERS

Thirty-one rigs, call it 900 watts each under load.  Say 28 kW continuous,
and continuous means continuous -- these things do not have a night.

Add cooling.  In this climate, in a room you do not own, budget 30 to 40 per
cent on top.  Call the whole thing 38 kW, drawn 24 hours a day, 8,760 hours a
year.

That is about 333,000 kilowatt-hours a year for one customer with thirty-one
machines in a garage.

Every customer we currently have, all six, all ten machines, comes to something
like 6 kW.


WHAT OUR PROVIDERS WOULD SAY

I have not asked them.  I do not need to, and I want to be clear that this is
from seven years inside facilities and not from a quote.

Mile High and Alder Street sell cabinets.  A cabinet comes with a power budget
-- commonly 3 to 5 kW, sometimes 8 if you pay for it and the room can carry it.
They price that way because the building was designed around an average, and
the average assumed servers that idle.

Gary wants 28 kW in the footprint of maybe two cabinets.  That is not a
cabinet you can buy from them.  It is six to nine cabinets' worth of power
delivered into two cabinets' worth of floor, which means different circuits,
different PDUs, and a cooling plan for that aisle specifically.

A facility will either say no, or say yes at a price that reflects the fact
that you are now the reason they are upgrading a room.

**Neither of those is a rejection of the idea.  Both mean the same thing: we
cannot do this inside the arrangements we have.**


WHAT WE WOULD NEED

If Cairnwell wanted to serve this customer properly:

    Our own space.  Not a cabinet in somebody's room.  A room, or a cage with
    its own power feed, with a landlord who has agreed in writing what we may
    draw.

    A power contract we understand.  Not "power is included".  Metered, with a
    demand charge, and we would need to know what the demand charge is before
    we quote anybody, because in a load like this it is most of the bill.

    Cooling that somebody has actually calculated.  Not a portable unit and a
    prayer.  I have watched two people try that and both of them ended up
    running the room with the door open in February.

    Electrical work by a licensed contractor, inspected.  This is where people
    get hurt and it is where a small company gets sued.

    A way to tell Gary his machines are off.  We have nothing that watches
    power.  Every check we own watches software.

I can price the first three inside a month.  I know who to telephone.


WHAT I WOULD SAY ABOUT THE CUSTOMER

Not my area and I am saying it anyway.

He is a good customer for a facility and a bad customer for us as we are
currently shaped.  He wants power, floor and silence.  He does not want
managed infrastructure and he would not pay us for it.

Everything Cairnwell is good at -- knowing what is on a machine, noticing a
backup failed, answering the telephone at eleven at night -- is worth nothing
to him.  His machines have no data on them.  If one dies he unplugs it.

So this would not be an extension of what we do.  It would be a second
business, sharing a name and some people.

That may well be worth doing.  It is not what John thinks he is buying.


ONE THING I WILL NOT LET PASS

John's note says Gary's bandwidth requirement is trivial and he was surprised.

He should be.  It means the only thing we would be selling is the building.
Everything that makes us hard to replace goes away, and we would be competing
on price per kilowatt with people who own their buildings.

I have worked for two of those people.  We would lose.
