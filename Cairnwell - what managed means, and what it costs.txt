CAIRNWELL TECHNOLOGIES
What "managed" means, and what it costs

Prepared by:  John Mercer
Reviewed by:  Alice Chen, Priya Raghunathan
Date:         9 January 2012
Status:       Internal.  Basis for the 2012 agreements.


WHY

Two things happened last year that were the same thing.

In July, Alice spent three days on Summit's web2 establishing what Mark had
deployed in 2009 and 2010.  In November, Priya spent three hours on Brightwater
establishing that a vendor we have never spoken to had broken a checkout page at
two in the morning.

Both were our work.  Both were correctly our work.  Neither was infrastructure,
and we billed for neither.

Four customers, nine machines, three people.  The fee covers running the
servers.  It has quietly also been covering an unlimited amount of finding out
whose fault something is, and finding out is the expensive part.


WHAT WE ARE NOT CHANGING

The sentence.  "We run your infrastructure so you don't have to hire someone to
do it."  It is why all four of them bought, it is still true, and I am not
narrowing it because it has become inconvenient.

The rule.  Application correctness is not ours.  When nobody knows what it is,
it is ours until we know, and then it goes where it belongs.  Alice wrote that
in April 2010 and it has survived every incident since including both of these.
Priya's words about November: "the arrangement working exactly as John described
it and I do not think anybody should change it."

What a customer hears when they telephone us.  Nobody is going to be asked to
diagnose their own problem before we will look at it.


WHAT WE ARE CHANGING

One thing.  The far side of the boundary gets a bill.

    We investigate anything, at any hour, as now.

    When the cause turns out to be application code or a third party, the
    first hour is included.  Beyond that we tell you we have passed it, what
    we think it is, and what further investigation will cost.  Then you
    decide.

    We tell you before we bill, not after.

That last line is not new.  Laura's attorney asked for it in May 2010 about
Mark's undisclosed changes, I agreed it was fair, and I wrote at the time that I
should have offered it.  It has been in Summit's agreement for eighteen months.
We are giving the other three the same terms rather than inventing new ones.

The first hour is deliberate.  Most of these end inside an hour, nobody gets a
bill, and nobody starts hesitating before telephoning us -- which is the thing
that would actually damage this business.


WHAT THIS MEANS PER CUSTOMER

    Summit          Mark deploys without telling us properly.  Already
                    covered by the existing clause.  This extends it from
                    changes he makes to causes that turn out to be his.

    Brightwater     The catalogue vendor.  We have no relationship with them
                    and cannot get one.  Dale has to be the one who telephones
                    them and now he knows that.

    Clear Creek     Ruth already understands this better than we do.  She is
                    also the only route to her own hosting provider and that
                    remains untested.

    Hartwell        The CMS is unmaintained and two versions behind.  We put
                    that in writing in January 2011.  When it breaks, and it
                    will, this is the clause that decides who pays for the
                    afternoon.


WHAT ALICE AND PRIYA ASKED FOR AND DID NOT GET

A list of what is in and out per customer, maintained.  Alice's argument is that
ambiguity is what costs us, not generosity.

I said no for now.  Four customers is not enough to need a catalogue and I do
not want to be the company whose answer to a telephone call is that it depends
what you bought.  Ask me again at eight customers, or the first time this clause
causes an argument with somebody, whichever comes first.

Alice's reply, recorded because she was right to make me write it down: "Then
write down that I asked, and when."


STILL NOT SETTLED

Pricing.  Four customers, four numbers, four different ways of arriving at
them, and now an hourly rate for investigation that I have set at $95 by
looking at what I used to charge in 2009 and adding nothing.  This is the third
year I have written that we need a rule.
