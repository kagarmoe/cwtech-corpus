CAIRNWELL TECHNOLOGIES
What would have to be true

Prepared by:  Alice Chen
Reviewed by:  Marcus Reyes
Date:         13 January 2014
Status:       Internal.  This is the document we will be judged against.


WHY THIS EXISTS

John asked me in December what would have to be true for three hundred
kilowatts to be a reasonable decision.  That was the right question and better
than the one I would have asked, which was whether I liked it.

I am writing the assumptions down, dated, in the order of how much they matter,
because in two years somebody — probably one of us — is going to want to know
whether this was stupid or unlucky, and those are different things.


WHAT I AM NOT DOING

Predicting the price of Bitcoin.  I have no idea.  Neither does John, neither
does Petra, and I have now read enough of what the loudest people in this field
write to be confident that they are guessing with more conviction than data.

Anybody reading this later should not take my caution as foresight.  I am not
cautious because I know something.  I am cautious because I do not.


THE ASSUMPTIONS, IN ORDER

**1.  The customers' commitments outlast ours.**

Three-year lease against three-year customer contracts.  That is the rule from
November and it is still the rule.

    What makes it false: a customer contract that is unenforceable in practice
    because the customer has no assets.  Halvard has money from a sold
    business and Petra has personal exposure.  Crestone I do not know.  We
    should know before we sign.

**2.  The space is lettable to somebody else.**

This is my March test and I have had to change it, and I want to be honest
that I have changed it rather than pretend it still says what it said.

    March: a room we would still want if the machines went in a skip.
    Now:   a room somebody would still want.

Three hundred kilowatts is more than Cairnwell's managed business will ever
use.  I cannot claim otherwise.  What I can claim is that a power-dense suite
in an established Denver facility, built to code, is a thing another tenant
will take — a rendering shop, a research group, anybody with dense compute.
There will not be nothing that wants power in Denver in 2017.

That is a weaker claim than the one I made in March.  It is still a claim I
believe.

    What makes it false: if the buildout is so specific to mining that it is
    unattractive to anyone else.  Marcus's job is to make sure it is not.  He
    says a suite is a suite and the specific part is the PDUs, which are
    movable.

**3.  The hardware being replaced does not make the room wrong.**

Marcus's objection from October, which I said did not change the decision and
which now might.

These people are buying machines that do only mining and they are denser.  If
the next generation is denser again, three hundred kilowatts spread over a
suite designed for today's density is the wrong shape — too much floor, not
enough power per rack, or the other way round.

    What makes it false: density doubling faster than we can re-provision.
    Marcus has designed for 12 kW a rack where we need 8 today, which he says
    buys us one generation and not two.

I want it recorded that Marcus raised this in October, that I dismissed it as
not changing the decision, and that he was more right than I was.  He has been
right about every facilities question since he arrived and I should weight him
accordingly.

**4.  The money survives a gap.**

If the suite is half full for six months we pay for it anyway.  I have asked
John for the number and it is about eleven thousand a month of exposure at
worst.

    What makes it false: less cash than we think, or a customer defaulting in
    the same quarter as a gap.  These are correlated, which is the part that
    worries me — they all depend on the same number, and I said that in
    October and it is still true and we are doing this anyway.

**5.  We can operate it.**

Not a business assumption, an honest one.  We are five people, one of whom
knows facilities, and we are about to have a suite that needs somebody in it.

    What makes it false: Marcus leaves, or gets ill, or we simply cannot
    recruit. This is a single point of failure with a name and a mortgage and
    we should say so.


WHAT I ACTUALLY THINK

I am for it.

Not because I believe in Bitcoin.  I have no opinion on Bitcoin and I would
rather not have one.

I am for it because the terms are matched, the asset is ordinary, and the
downside is eleven thousand a month for a year rather than the company.  If I
am wrong about assumption two I am wrong expensively, and if I am wrong about
assumption one I am wrong fatally, and I think assumption one is the one to
spend our lawyer on.

John and I are agreeing to the same thing for different reasons.  He thinks the
demand is real.  I think the building is real.  Both of us could be right, or
he could be right and I could be wrong, and the one that kills us is if I am
right and he is wrong, because then we have a lovely empty suite.

I would rather that were funny.
