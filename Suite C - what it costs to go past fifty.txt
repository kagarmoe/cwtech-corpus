NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
Suite C -- what it costs to go past fifty

Prepared by:  Marcus Reyes
Date:         20 November 2018
Status:       Internal

John asked for the price of the thing I said in August I could price and nobody
had asked me to.  Here it is.

It took a fortnight and it is not the number I expected to be writing about.


THE WORK ITSELF

To recap what I wrote in August.  The room is 44 racks.  29 are occupied and 26
of those hold something that is still a customer.  Measured draw 147 kW.  We
designed for 12 kW a rack in 2014 and machines people buy now want 17 to 20.

So we have floor we cannot power and power we cannot put anywhere.

Fifty kilowatts is what I can give somebody today in racks that are already fed.
Past that, every rack needs more circuit than it has.

    Thirteen racks brought from 12 kW to 20 kW
      New PDUs, two per rack, metered              $11,400
      Circuits and whips, thirteen racks           $23,800
      Panel work and a subpanel in C               $18,500
      Electrician, permit, inspection              $14,200
      Containment adjustment where racks change     $3,600
      Contingency, fifteen per cent                $10,700

      Total                                        $82,200

Six to eight weeks from order, and that assumes the electrical contractor I have
used twice can start when he says he can.  Two of those weeks are the facility's
and not mine -- the feed into C comes off their distribution and they have to do
their side before I can do mine.

That buys us to about 200 kW.  Not 300.  300 is the number in the lease and it
has never been a real number for the hardware people actually buy.

    Done live, around customers who are paying, at night, one rack at a time.
    I have done this before in somebody else's room and it is slow and it is
    not dangerous if you do not hurry it.


AND THEN JOHN ASKED ME HOW LONG WE HAVE GOT THE ROOM FOR

I could not answer that either, which I did not expect, and it is the reason
this document is late.

The 2014 agreement is three years from 1 April 2014.  That is in "the suite,
what we signed" and I wrote it.  It ran out on 31 March 2017 and we have been in
the room for twenty months since.

I telephoned Alder Street and asked what they think we are on.  Their property
manager is a woman called Renata Kovac who has been there longer than we have.
She read it to me off her file and sent it over the same afternoon.

Alice then found our copy, in the filing cabinet, in the folder marked 2017.

    First renewal, signed 19 January 2017.
    Three years from 1 April 2017, ending 31 March 2020.
    Committed floor unchanged at 180 kW.  Ceiling unchanged at 300 kW.
    One option to extend, five years, at market rate, exercisable by written
    notice not later than 30 September 2019.

So we have sixteen months of certainty and an option nobody has priced.

Alice remembers signing it.  She says the reason it was three and not five was
that in January 2017 we had lost Vail and Gary, Crestone were using a third of
what they paid for, and she did not want to be holding a five-year floor into a
room she was not sure we could fill.  That was the right call with what she knew
and it is the call that is now in our way.

    I want to be plain about my own part.  I negotiated that renewal with Renata
    and I did not put the term in any document anybody here reads.  It went in a
    folder.  When John went looking in November he could not find it, and he is
    the one who has to sell against it.

    Twenty months.  Nobody asked and I did not say.


WHAT THAT DOES TO THE FOURMILE QUESTION

Fourmile want three years and would take five.  Three years from, say, February
2019 runs to February 2022.

We hold the room to 31 March 2020.

    Sixteen months of what they want to buy, and then an option at a price
    nobody has quoted, which we must decide about by 30 September 2019.

I am not the person to say what to do about that.  What I can say is the part
that is mine:

    Eighty-two thousand and six to eight weeks gets us to 200 kW.
    Fifty kilowatts is available now without spending anything.
    Neither number is affected by the lease.  The spending is.

Eighty-two thousand of PDUs, circuits and panel work goes into a room we hold
for sixteen more months.  The PDUs come out and travel.  The circuits, the
subpanel and the containment do not.  Call it thirty thousand that walks and
fifty that stays in somebody else's building.

That is not my decision and I am not going to dress it up as an engineering
question.  It is Alice's.


WHAT I HAVE NOT DONE

Not ordered anything.  Not asked Renata what the option costs, because asking
puts us on a clock I have not been authorised to start.

Not looked at whether there is space elsewhere in that building.  John asked and
I said I would not go looking until we know what we are doing, because Renata
will hear about it the same week and I would rather negotiate from where we are
than from where she thinks we might be going.
