NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
Fourmile want seventy kilowatts

Prepared by:  John Mercer
Date:         5 November 2018
Status:       Internal


One of the ten from August has come back and it is serious.


WHO

Fourmile Geoscience, Lakewood.  About sixty people.  They do subsurface
analysis, engineering computation and visualisation for energy companies --
their clients are operators, and Fourmile are the people those operators pay to
tell them what is under the ground and what it is worth doing about it.

Their principal is Ellen Voss.  Their machines are run by a man called Dinesh
Rajagopalan, whose title is IT manager and who plainly does a good deal more
than that.  I have met them both twice.

They can pay.  They offered me two years of statements before I asked, which I
have not often seen, and I have spoken to their bank.

That is as much as I know about their business and as much as I need to know.
Dinesh was clear that the work itself is not ours and I did not press him on it.


WHAT THEY HAVE, AND WHY THEY ARE MOVING

Fourteen machines with GPUs in them, bought over four years.  Nine sit in a
converted storeroom at their office.  Five are in a half cabinet at a facility
in Denver.

They want them in one place.  Dinesh has wanted the storeroom emptied for a
long time and something has now made it urgent enough to act on.

They are buying more.  That is the part that matters to us.


WHAT THEY ARE ASKING FOR

    About seventy kilowatts to start with
    Their hardware, not ours
    Racking it, powering it, cooling it, replacing it when it dies
    A connection in and out that is ours and not a broadband line
    Power and temperature monitoring
    Hands, including at night
    Room to add more during the term

    A term of years, not a rolling arrangement
    And -- this is the one -- they want us to tell them how much MORE we can
    give them, on what schedule, under what responsibilities, and at what price

They are not asking us to run anything.  Dinesh runs the machines and the
software and does not want us near either.  He said that twice and I liked him
for it.


WHY THIS IS NOT LIKE THE OTHERS

Seventy is more than the fifty Marcus told me in August we can do today.

And they are not asking for seventy.  They are asking for seventy AND a number
for later, in writing, in a contract, for years.

I have sold infrastructure for twenty-six years and I have never been asked to
put future capacity in a contract by a company this size.  Ellen has been on
the other end of a supplier who could not deliver what he promised and she was
explicit that she is not doing that again.

    This is the most money anybody has ever offered us in one conversation.

I want to be careful about that sentence.  I wrote in August that I am the wrong
person to say whether the market has changed, and I am equally the wrong person
to say whether this particular deal is a good one.

I will say one thing that is not about them.  Dev's render nodes, Dr Ma's
machines and these fourteen do not have one thing in common as businesses --
one makes films, one sequences, one looks under Wyoming -- and they want almost
exactly the same thing from us.  I do not know what to make of that and I am
recording it rather than concluding from it.


WHAT I HAVE NOT PROMISED

Anything.

I said fifty today, that more is possible and costs money, and that I would come
back with a number and a date.  Same as August.  No year, no price.


WHAT I NEED

From Marcus: what it costs to go past fifty, and how long it takes.  He said in
August he could price it and nobody had asked.  I am asking.

From Alice: whether we can sign a term with anybody at all.  Ellen wants three
years and would take five.

    I do not know how long we have got Suite C for.  I went to look and could
    not find it, which I was not expecting.  The 2014 document says three years
    from April 2014.  That ran out eighteen months ago and we are plainly still
    in the room.

    Somebody knows.  I do not.
