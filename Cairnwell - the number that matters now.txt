CAIRNWELL TECHNOLOGIES
The number that matters now

Prepared by:  John Mercer
Date:         16 March 2015
Status:       Internal


WHERE WE ARE

    Committed floor, Alder Street       180 kW
    Billing as of 1 March               101 kW
    Drawing                              88 kW

We are paying for seventy-nine kilowatts nobody is buying.  That is a little
over eleven thousand dollars a month and it is Alice's January 2014 worst case,
arriving fourteen months late and looking exactly like she said it would.

We have twenty-three months left on the lease.


HOW WE GOT FROM 152 TO 101

**Crestone.**  Stopped paying in January.  They contracted sixty and used
twenty-four and never got the hardware they ordered in early 2014.  Their
position is that they were sold capacity for machines that a vendor took their
money for and never shipped, and that it is unreasonable to hold them to it.

Our position is that we built a room on the strength of their signature.

Both of those are true.  I have a contract and I do not have a company worth
suing, and I have now spent enough time with their books to know that.

Settled on 10 March: they pay eighteen thousand over nine months and walk.  It
is about a fifth of what the contract says.  I took it because the alternative
was a lawyer, eighteen months and probably nothing.

**Vail.**  Did not renew in February.  No drama, no default.  They said the
economics do not work at this price and thanked us.  Their machines came out in
a weekend and Toby had them boxed and gone by the Tuesday.

**Lindqvist.**  Gary gave notice in December.  His last rigs left in January.
He is out of it entirely and back to the web shop.  He sent Alice a bottle of
something and a note saying it had been the most interesting three years of his
working life, which I have kept.

**Halvard.**  Unchanged.  Ninety-six kilowatts, pays on the first, two years
left.  Petra has been the only good news in this document.


WHAT I HAVE DONE ABOUT IT

**Alder Street.**  Asked to reduce the floor.  They said no, and they were
pleasant about it, and they were right to say no — the floor is what paid for
their buildout.

Asked instead whether they would take a longer term at a lower floor.  Five
years at 120 kW instead of two more at 180.  They are considering it.  I do not
know whether I want them to say yes, and I have told Alice that I do not know,
which is not a sentence I enjoy.

**The line.**  Sixty thousand against receivables.  Receivables are smaller, so
the line is smaller.  Thirty-eight available, twenty-two drawn.  The bank has
not done anything unreasonable and I would rather they never have to.

**Everything else.**  The managed business — Summit, Clear Creek, Brightwater,
Hartwell, Larkspur, Cortez — has not moved.  Same customers, same invoices,
paid the same as ever.  In the middle of all this it has been the only part of
the company that behaves like a business.

I want that noticed, because for eighteen months I have treated it as the thing
we did before the interesting thing.


WHAT I HAVE NOT DONE

Let anybody go.

I raised it.  Toby is the newest and the cheapest and I said so out loud in
February, and I want it written down that I said it because I do not want to be
remembered as the one who did not think of it.

Alice's answer: that if we cut the person whose job is the room, we are deciding
the room has no future, and that we should decide that on purpose if we are
going to decide it.

She is right.  We have not decided that.  So he stays and I find the money
elsewhere.


WHAT I AM ASKING FOR

Alice and Marcus to tell me what we have that is worth something to somebody who
has never heard of Bitcoin.

Not what we could build.  What we have.
