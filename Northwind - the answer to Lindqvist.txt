NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
The answer to Lindqvist

Prepared by:  John Mercer
Reviewed by:  Alice Chen, Marcus Reyes
Date:         11 March 2013
Status:       Internal


WHAT WE TOLD HIM

No, for now, with the reasons.

I telephoned Gary on the 8th and told him we cannot host thirty-one machines at
28 kilowatts inside the arrangements we have, that doing it properly means our
own space and our own power contract, and that we are not going to sign a lease
on the strength of one customer.

He took it well.  He said two other people had told him no without explaining
anything, and that the explanation was worth the fortnight.

I asked him to keep us informed about what he ends up doing.  He will.


WHAT ALICE SAID, WHICH I AM RECORDING IN FULL

She has no objection to the infrastructure problem.  Her words: "It is the most
interesting thing anyone has asked us to do and I would like to do it."

Her objection is to the shape of the commitment.

    The customer's ability to pay depends on the price of a thing neither he
    nor we control.  We have never had that before.  Every customer we have
    sells tickets or plumbing or drawings, and if the price of those moves ten
    per cent it does not change whether they can pay us.

    The hardware is changing underneath him.  Gary himself says there are
    machines coming that do only this and do it far faster, and that nobody
    knows when they arrive or what they cost.  The rigs we would be cooling
    might be worthless before a lease is a year old.

    What we would be building -- a room with power and cooling -- is useful
    for exactly one thing if it is built for exactly this.  Built for general
    purposes it is expensive.  Built for mining it is a bet.

    "I am not saying no.  I am saying that if we do this, we do it with a room
    that would still be worth having if every one of these machines went in a
    skip."

That last sentence is the useful one and I have written it on the whiteboard.

For the avoidance of doubt: she is not predicting the machines go in a skip.
She said explicitly that she has no idea and neither does anyone else.


WHAT MARCUS SAID THAT I HAD NOT CONSIDERED

That if all the customer wants is power and floor, the only thing we would be
selling is the building -- and we would be competing on price per kilowatt with
people who own theirs.  He has worked for two of them.  His view is we would
lose.

I think he is right and it is the argument that actually decided this, more
than the money.

Everything we are good at is worth nothing to Gary.  He does not need us to
know what is on his machines, because there is nothing on them.


WHAT WE ARE DOING ANYWAY

Not nothing.  Three things, all cheap.

    Marcus prices space and power.  Not to sign anything -- so that the next
    time somebody asks, we can answer in an afternoon instead of a fortnight.
    He says he can do it inside a month and knows who to telephone.

    We find out whether there are really four more of these in Colorado or
    whether Gary knows four people who know each other.  One customer is a
    customer.  Five is a market and a different conversation.

    Marcus writes down what we would need to watch a room rather than a
    server -- power draw, temperature, whether a machine is on.  We have
    nothing that does any of that.  Every check we own watches software.

None of this costs more than his time and my telephone calls.


WHAT I WANT ON THE RECORD

I was keen and I am still keen.  Alice and Marcus both told me things I did not
know, and what changed my mind was Marcus's point about selling the building,
which is not an objection I would have reached on my own.

I have written down that I was keen, so that if this comes back and I am keen
again, somebody can tell me I was keen the first time too.
