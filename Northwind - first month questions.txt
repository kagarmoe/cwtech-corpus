QUESTIONS, WEEK ONE TO FOUR

Priya Raghunathan
Started 11 April 2011.  Written up 6 May.

Alice told me to keep a list instead of asking the same thing twice.  This is
the list.  Answers are hers unless it says otherwise.

Some of these have no answer yet.  I have left those in.


ABOUT THE ESTATE

1.  How many machines do we manage?
    Eight, across four customers.  Nine if you count mon1, and you should.

2.  Where is the authoritative list?
    mon1:/usr/local/nwcheck/records, one file per customer.  Read the README
    first.

3.  The records say what a machine is.  Where do I find out what to do when
    one of them alerts?
    Nowhere.  Alice knows.  That is the problem she hired me to have.

4.  Which customers can I log into?
    All of them.  My key went on everything in week one.  Alice did it while
    I watched, which I appreciated more than she probably realised.

5.  Is there anywhere that says what we promised each customer?
    The OURS / NOT OURS / RESPONSE sections of each record.  Read them before
    you tell a customer anything.  Alice was emphatic about this and I now
    know why -- see 21.


ABOUT ALERTS

6.  How many checks are there?
    Thirty-one across the four customers.

7.  How many have ever fired?
    Alice could name four.  I went through the mail archive.  Eleven have
    fired at least once since May 2010.  Twenty have never fired.

8.  So do the other twenty work?
    Unknown.  They passed their tests when they were written.  Nothing has
    exercised them against a real failure.  Alice's words: "that is a fair
    question and I do not have a good answer."

9.  What happens if a check is wrong and reports OK forever?
    Nothing.  We would not know.

10. What wakes somebody up?
    Mail to Alice.  There is no paging.  Out of hours the customer telephones
    and that is the actual alerting mechanism.

11. Which alerts are worth waking Alice for at 2am?
    This is what I am writing the alerts file for.  Her answer was a
    twenty-minute conversation and it needs to be four lines per check.


THE ONE THAT WORRIED ME

12. What alerts us if mon1 stops?
    Nothing.

13. Nothing at all?
    Nothing at all.  mon1 runs every check for every customer, holds the
    customer records, and receives Hartwell & Reed's nightly backups.  If it
    stops, all monitoring stops silently, we lose the records except for the
    copy on Alice's workstation, and Hartwell's backups stop without anybody
    noticing.

14. How long has that been true?
    Since May 2010.  Alice knew.  She wrote "mon1 is becoming load bearing and
    it is a $28 virtual server" in the Hartwell record in January and then
    carried on.

15. Why did nobody fix it?
    Her answer: because she would have noticed.  Which was true when she was
    the only person and is the kind of true that stops being true quietly.

    We fixed part of it on 3 May.  See the alerts file.  It is a partial fix
    and it is written down as one.


ABOUT WHAT I AM ALLOWED TO DO

16. Can I restart a service on a customer machine without asking?
    Yes, if it is one of ours and it is down.  Tell Alice afterward.  Write
    it in the record.

17. Can I make a configuration change?
    Not on your own, not yet.  Ask.  This will change.

18. Can I tell a customer what is wrong?
    You can tell them what you are doing.  Do not tell them what caused it
    until we know.  John was specific about this and pointed me at something
    he wrote in July about the difference.

19. When do I escalate?
    Alice's first answer was "use your judgment", which is not usable at 2am
    in month two.  Her second answer, after I said that, is in the alerts
    file per check.

20. Who do I call at each customer out of hours?
    This was not written down anywhere.  It is now, in each record, under
    CONTACT.  Hartwell & Reed did not have an out-of-hours contact at all;
    Cheryl gave me her mobile on 28 April and was slightly alarmed to be
    asked.


THE ONE I GOT WRONG

21. Brightwater rang on 26 April about the catalogue being slow.  I said we
    would look at their database backup while we were in there.  Dale said
    "you don't do our backups."  He was right.  I had read Summit's record
    that morning and Brightwater's a week earlier.

    Nobody was harmed and Dale was decent about it.  But this is the same
    mistake John made in December with the same customer about the same
    thing, four months apart, by two different people, for the same reason.

    Alice's note when I told her: "Then it is not a John problem."
