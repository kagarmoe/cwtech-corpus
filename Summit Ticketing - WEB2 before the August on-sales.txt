NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
Summit Ticketing -- WEB2, before the August on-sales

Prepared by:  Alice Chen
Date:         July 25, 2011
Status:       Internal

Laura asked on the 18th whether WEB2 was ready to take traffic for the August
run.  Routine question, asked in good time, and the honest answer took three
days to produce.


WHAT WE EXPECTED TO FIND

From the environment review of April 2010, and unchanged in the record since:

    A second copy of part of the application.  Eleven files behind WEB1.
    Mark updates WEB1 first and WEB2 later if he expects to use it.

That is what the record says.  Nobody has looked at it since I wrote it, which
is fifteen months.


WHAT IS ACTUALLY THERE

Not eleven files behind.  Not behind in any consistent way.

    Of 214 application files on WEB2:

        168  identical to WEB1
         31  older than WEB1
          9  NEWER than WEB1
          6  present on WEB2 and not on WEB1 at all

The nine newer files are the interesting ones.  Mark pushed to WEB2 during the
Red Rocks run last August, in a hurry, and pushed to WEB1 afterward -- but not
all of it.  Two of those nine are in the order path.

The six that exist only on WEB2 are older than that.  Four are from a
contractor who worked on the payment integration in 2009; the other two Mark
does not recognise.


WHAT THE EVIDENCE ACTUALLY SUPPORTS

I want to be careful here because the evidence is weak and it would be easy to
write a confident story.

    Established.  The files differ, in the counts above, as of 22 July 2011.
    That is a direct comparison and it is not in doubt.

    Established.  Mark deployed to WEB2 on or about 12 August 2010.  He
    remembers doing it and the Apache logs for that week show WEB2 serving
    order traffic, which it otherwise never does.

    Not established.  Which specific files went in that deployment.  I have
    modification times and nothing else.  There is no source control at Summit
    and there never has been.  An mtime tells me when a file was last written
    and not what was in it or who wrote it, and several of these files have
    mtimes that cannot be right -- three claim to predate the server they are
    on, which happens when files are copied without preserving times, which is
    exactly how Mark deploys.

    Not established.  Whether the two newer files in the order path ever ran
    against real customers.  If WEB2 took order traffic in August 2010 then
    probably yes.  Probably is not a finding.

    Unknown, and likely to stay unknown.  What the two unrecognised files do.
    Mark did not write them and the contractor who might have is not
    contactable.  They are not referenced from anything I can find.  That is
    not the same as being unused.


WHAT I DID NOT DO

I did not make WEB2 match WEB1.

That was my first instinct and it is wrong.  Two of the newer files are in the
order path, which means WEB1 may be the one that is behind, and overwriting
them would be destroying the only copy of a change Mark made under pressure a
year ago and has not thought about since.  I am not doing that during selling
season on the strength of a file comparison.

I did not delete the six orphans either, for the reason we have now learned
twice -- once with the summitftp account and once with Mark's deploy key.
Nobody knowing what something is for is not the same as nothing using it.


WHAT WE DID

Told Laura WEB2 can take overflow traffic for images and static content, which
is what it actually does for her during a run, and that we are not confident
enough in the application copy to send order traffic to it.

She asked whether that was a change.  It is not.  It has been true since at
least August 2010 and nobody knew.

Mark has the file list.  He is going to reconcile the nine and the six after
the season, which is the right time to do it and also the time at which people
stop caring.  I have put a note in the record with a date on it.


WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT US

The record said eleven files.  It said eleven files in April 2010 because that
was true in April 2010, and it went on saying eleven files for fifteen months
because a record that nobody reads against the system is a record of what
somebody once saw.

LAST REVIEWED exists in these files for exactly this reason and I put it there
myself.  Summit's said 20 April 2011, three months ago, and it was still wrong,
because reviewing a record is not the same as checking it against the machine
and I have not been honest with myself about the difference.

I am not proposing a process.  I am proposing that when a file says something
specific and checkable about a system -- a file count, a version, a threshold --
somebody checks it, and that the checkable things are marked so we know which
they are.
