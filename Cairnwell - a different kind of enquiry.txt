CAIRNWELL TECHNOLOGIES
A different kind of enquiry

Prepared by:  John Mercer
Date:         19 February 2013
Status:       Internal


THE CALL

Gary Lindqvist telephoned on the 14th.  He came through Ed Sasaki, who was a
colocation customer of mine at FrontRange in about 2007.

Gary runs a small web shop in Boulder.  That is not what he wants to talk about.

He has thirty-one machines mining Bitcoin in a nine-hundred-square-foot
industrial unit and he wants somebody else to host them.  He has tripped the
building's supply twice.  His landlord has started asking what he is doing in
there.

I had to have most of it explained to me twice and I am recording what I
understood rather than what he said, so that Alice can correct the parts I have
wrong.

    The machines do one arithmetic problem, continuously, forever.  There is
    no idle.  A web server is busy in bursts; these are busy always.

    They are bought for how fast they compute and they are run until they are
    not worth the electricity, at which point they are switched off.  Gary
    talks about his machines the way somebody talks about a truck they intend
    to run into the ground.

    What he pays for is power.  Not space, not bandwidth -- he says his
    bandwidth is trivial, which surprised me.  Power, and getting the heat out.

    He has a spreadsheet with a column called "$/MH/day" which he knows to
    four decimal places.  He could not tell me his web shop's gross margin.

Bandwidth being trivial is the part I keep returning to.  Every customer we
have ever had bought a server so that other people could reach it.  His
machines do not need to be reached.  They need to be powered and cooled and
otherwise left alone.

That is not a variation on our business.  That is a different business that
happens to involve racks.


WHY I AM INTERESTED

Because he is not the only one.  He mentioned four other people in Colorado
doing the same thing at the same scale and said the mailing lists are full of
people asking where to put machines.  I have not verified any of that.

Because the thing he actually wants -- somebody competent to take the
electrical and thermal problem off him so he can get on with the part he cares
about -- is a sentence away from the one I have been selling since 2010.

And because he is paying somebody for power right now, and it is not us.


WHAT I DO NOT KNOW

Whether any of this lasts.  I said that to him and he was not offended; he said
it is the first question everybody asks and that he does not know either.

I remember Bitcoin being in the news in 2011 and then not being in the news.
Alice looked it up: it went from about thirty dollars to about two dollars in
five months that year.  It is around thirty again now.

I am not putting that in front of Gary as an argument.  I am putting it here
because if we build something for this and it goes away, we will have built it
with money we do not have.


WHAT I HAVE ASKED FOR

Marcus to tell me what it would actually take.  Not whether we should.  What it
would take.

Alice to tell me what I have got wrong in the four points above.

I have told Gary we will come back to him in a fortnight and that I do not yet
know whether the answer is yes.
