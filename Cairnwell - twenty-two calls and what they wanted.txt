CAIRNWELL TECHNOLOGIES
Twenty-two calls and what they wanted

Prepared by:  John Mercer
Date:         6 August 2018
Status:       Internal


In April 2010 I made fourteen calls before we had a services draft and wrote up
what I heard.  I have not done it since.  I am doing it again because the calls
have stopped sounding the same as each other and I want the change written down
by somebody who is not guessing at it afterwards.

Twenty-two conversations since the start of April.  Eleven came to me, eleven I
went after.


THE SPLIT

    Ordinary managed infrastructure          9
    Machines, power, hands, capacity        10
    Neither, or I could not tell             3

Nine and ten.  In 2013 it would have been eighteen and one.


THE ORDINARY NINE

Small Linux estates.  Somebody's web application, a database, backups nobody
has tested, monitoring that means a customer telephones.  This is our business
and I know how to sell it.

Six of the nine asked me, unprompted, why they would use us instead of Amazon.

I did not have a good answer.

I have been selling infrastructure for twenty-six years and I have always had
an answer to "why you."  This time I said things that were true -- that we
answer the telephone, that we will look at their whole environment and not just
the part we sold them, that nobody at Amazon knows their application is called
confirm.php.  Two of them found that persuasive.  Four were polite.

    I want to be careful here, because I am the wrong person to ask whether
    this is a market change or a sales problem.  I have a direct interest in
    it being a market change.

Three of the nine are worth following up.  Two of those three are smaller than
Hartwell, which is the smallest customer we have ever taken.


THE OTHER TEN

These did not sound like our business.  They sounded like Suite C.

What they had in common, roughly:

    They already own machines, or are about to buy them
    The machines are dense -- more power per rack than anything
        we ran before 2014
    They want somebody to rack them, power them, cool them, and
        put hands on them
    They are not asking us to run an operating system
    Several were explicit that we should not look inside the work

Four of the ten asked what our capacity is.

I could not tell them.

I said I would find out and come back.  That is the honest answer and it is
also not an answer, and one of the four had already been quoted by somebody
else by the time I came back to him.

    Marcus, this is the ask.  Not "how much room is there" in general.  Four
    people asked me a number and a date and I had neither.


THE THREE I COULD NOT PLACE

One wants us to run something on a public cloud on his behalf, which is a
different business from either of the above and which I told him we do not do.

One is a hardware reseller who wants to put his customers in our room, which
would make us somebody's subcontractor and which I have not thought about
properly.

One I genuinely did not understand and he did not either.


WHAT I AM NOT SAYING

I am not saying we should become a hosting company.  I have watched this
company decide one thing at a time for eight years and it has worked.

I am not saying the managed business is finished.  Four customers who have been
with us since 2010 and 2011 pay us every month and nothing about them has
changed.

I am not saying the ten calls are a market.  Ten calls is ten calls.  In
September 2013 I wrote up four mining conversations and said they might be
nothing, and Alice wrote back that we should find out what we would need to be
true.  That was the right answer then and I expect it is the right answer now.

What I am saying is that I could not answer a question four people asked me,
and that is mine to raise and not mine to solve.


WHAT I WOULD LIKE

A number I can say out loud.

How much can we take, of the dense kind, and how quickly.  Not a study.  A
number and a date, of the sort I can put in a sentence on a telephone without
telling somebody something that turns out not to be true.

I would rather have a small honest number than a large one I have to walk back.
