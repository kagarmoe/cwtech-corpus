CAIRNWELL TECHNOLOGIES
The January assumptions, checked

Prepared by:  Alice Chen
Date:         20 October 2014
Status:       Internal

I wrote five assumptions in January and said they were the document we would be
judged against.  Marcus has written where the suite actually is.  This is me
going through my own list rather than waiting for somebody else to do it.

I am doing it now, at nine months, because at nine months I still remember what
I meant.


**1.  The customers' commitments outlast ours.**

Holding.  Halvard is three years and pays.  Crestone and Vail are two years and
pay, and both are using less than they contracted, which is the assumption
working rather than failing -- that is what a commitment is for.

The thing I did not think about: a customer who pays for more than they use is
a customer who is unhappy while paying.  Crestone is current on 60 kW and using
24 and will remember that at renewal.  My assumption was about whether they
could pay.  It should also have been about whether they would want to again.

**2.  The space is lettable to somebody else.**

Untested, and I am the reason.

I argued in January that a power-dense suite in an established Denver facility
would be wanted by somebody -- a rendering shop, a research group, anybody with
dense compute.  We have thirteen empty racks.

Nobody has asked us.  We have also not asked anybody.  In nine months we have
not made one approach to a non-mining tenant, because John has been selling
mining and I have been running the company and neither of us did the thing I
said in January was the whole reason this was safe.

    That is not the assumption failing.  That is us not testing it.  It is
    worse, because a failed assumption teaches you something and this teaches
    us nothing until somebody picks up a telephone.

**3.  Hardware replacement does not make the room the wrong shape.**

**Failed.**  Marcus has the detail and it is exactly what he said in October
last year.

We have power we cannot sell and floor we cannot fill, because the machines
people buy now want 17 to 20 kW a rack and we built for 12.

What I said in January was that he was more right than I was and that I should
weight him accordingly.  I would like to think I did.  In practice, weighting
him accordingly in February would have meant spending money on density we could
not demonstrate a need for, and I do not believe I would have signed that and I
do not think I would have been wrong not to.

    So the honest version is: he identified the risk, we bought one generation
    against it, and one generation was not enough.  That is a bad outcome from
    a reasonable process, and I would rather record it that way than pretend
    either that we were blindsided or that we blundered.

**4.  The money survives a gap.**

Holding, comfortably.  Four thousand a month of exposure against a worst case
of eleven.

I notice I am pleased about this and I should not be too pleased.  The reason
the number is four and not eleven is that Crestone and Vail are paying for
capacity they are not using.  When those contracts come up, the number moves.

**5.  We can operate it.**

Holding, and it is the one that frightens me.

Marcus wrote in February that somebody would need to be in that suite most days
and that it could not always be him, and asked that we hire before we were
desperate.  It is October.  We have not hired.

    He has not complained.  He has been there most days.

    I am putting this at the top of the list for the next quarter, and I am
    recording that he asked in writing in February and that the delay is mine.


WHAT I CONCLUDE

We are not in trouble.  We are in the uncomfortable middle, which is where most
decisions actually end up and where nobody writes anything down because there
is no story in it.

One assumption failed, one is holding for a reason that will expire, one is
untested because we did not test it, and one is holding because a man is doing
too much and not saying so.

    Stupid or unlucky: unlucky on three, and on two -- the lettability -- we
    have not earned the right to say, because we have not tried.

The next thing I do is not about the suite.  It is picking up a telephone and
finding out whether anybody in Denver wants thirteen racks.
