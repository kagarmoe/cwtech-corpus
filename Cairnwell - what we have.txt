CAIRNWELL TECHNOLOGIES
What we have

Prepared by:  Alice Chen
Contributions from: Marcus Reyes, Priya Raghunathan
Date:         15 June 2015
Status:       Internal

John asked what we have that is worth something to somebody who has never heard
of Bitcoin.  Not what we could build.  What we have.

This is a list and not a plan.  I have deliberately not drawn a conclusion from
it, because the four times this company has got into difficulty it has been
because somebody had the answer before they had the list.


THE PHYSICAL THINGS

**A compliant 300 kW suite with hot aisle containment, twenty-three months left,
in a facility people want to be in.**

Marcus built it deliberately general.  It is not a mining room.  It is a room.
It is currently 88 kW used and 44 racks of which 19 are occupied.

    What is wrong with it: it is provisioned at 12 kW a rack.  Anything wanting
    more than that needs distribution work Marcus has priced at nineteen
    thousand for a twelve-rack block.

    What is right with it: containment, metering, code-compliant, inspected,
    and somebody else's building to maintain.

**Nothing else physical.**  mon1 is a virtual server costing twenty-eight
dollars a month.  We own no hardware of consequence.  We never did.  That was
deliberate and it is the reason we are having an uncomfortable year instead of
a fatal one.


THE THINGS WE KNOW HOW TO DO

Marcus and Toby wrote this part and I have not softened it.

    **Running a dense room.**  Power, cooling, containment, airflow, thermal
    monitoring, capacity against a committed floor.  Two years of doing it
    without an incident.  Cairnwell did not know any of this in 2013.

    **Remote hands at speed.**  Toby has done about four hundred and fifty
    physical interventions in eighteen months.  Racking, cabling, swaps,
    escorting, boxing a customer's machines when they leave.  We are good at
    this and we were not good at it in 2013 because we had never done it.

    **Dealing with providers as an equal.**  Marcus can get an answer out of a
    facility in an afternoon.  We could not do that in 2012 — the Clear Creek
    hardware route sat untested for two years because nobody knew how.

    **Operating machines we do not understand and are not responsible for.**
    This one is Priya's and I think it is the most interesting on the list.
    Mining taught us to watch a machine's liveness and power and temperature
    while knowing nothing about what it computes and having no opinion about
    whether it is doing well.  Every other customer we have, we understand
    their software.  For three years we have run a business line where we
    deliberately did not.

**And the thing we have always had:** six managed customers who have paid every
month through all of this, some since 2010.  Records, runbooks, a deferred
list, thirty-odd checks and the discipline to keep them current.  That business
did not move an inch while the interesting one fell over.


THE THINGS WE THOUGHT WE HAD AND DO NOT

    **A 300 kW ceiling.**  Practically about 200 for anything modern.  We have
    stopped quoting the big number.

    **A reputation in mining.**  We have one, and it is worth exactly what a
    good reputation is worth in an industry that is contracting.  Petra would
    recommend us.  There is nobody to recommend us to.

    **Assumption two.**  In October I wrote that we had never tested whether
    anyone else wants the room.  Since then Priya and I have made eleven
    approaches — rendering, a genomics group at the university, two engineering
    firms, a video post house, a backup provider.

    Nine said no.  Two are talking to us.  Neither is large.

    That is not a market.  It is also not nothing, and it is the first real
    information we have had on a question I said in January 2014 was the whole
    reason this was safe.  I should have made those calls eighteen months ago.


WHAT I AM NOT DOING IN THIS DOCUMENT

Recommending anything.

There is an obvious-sounding sentence available about how the capability might
outlast the market, and I am not writing it, because I have watched John and
myself reach for obvious-sounding sentences twice in three years and I would
like us to look at the list for a fortnight first.

The one thing I will say: **none of the five things on the capability list
depends on Bitcoin existing.**  Whether any of them is worth money to anybody is
a separate question and this document does not answer it.
