Laura,

Thank you for your patience last night, and for the twenty minutes on the
phone when I did not have much to tell you. Here is the fuller answer, and
what we have already changed.


WHAT HAPPENED

Between about 7:20 and 8:34 last night, during the Boulder Theater on-sale,
some customers trying to buy tickets got an error instead of a confirmation.
Both servers were up the entire time, which is part of why it took us a
few minutes to find.

The cause was a specific query on the confirmation page -- the part that
looks up a customer's recent orders -- running slowly against a large table
under load, which briefly queued up other people's purchases behind it. Mark
had made a small change to that page earlier in the evening; he identified
it, rolled it back, and purchases were completing normally again by 8:34.

We do not have a record of every purchase that failed. We are looking at
whether there is a way to get one.


WHAT WE CHANGED THIS MORNING

We added a check that watches how many database connections are in use, so
we hear about a problem like this minutes earlier next time, before it
affects customers rather than after.

We also turned on more detailed database logging, so if something like this
happens again we can tell you what query was involved without having to
reconstruct it from memory the next morning.

We fixed a flaw in our own alerting: during the incident it sent us more
than twenty emails in an hour instead of one, which is not useful to anyone
and has been corrected.


WHAT WE HAVEN'T DONE

We are not adding an index to fix the underlying query during your selling
season, because doing that locks the table it is on for the time it takes to
build, and we are not willing to cause a second outage to prevent this one
from happening again. It is on the list for after the season.

We also don't yet have a way to test the actual purchase path from outside,
the way a customer would experience it, without generating real orders.
Alice is working out a safe way to do that with Mark.


ONE THING WORTH REPEATING

You asked me in April how we would tell the difference between our problem
and Mark's. Last night is the clearest example we have had. We investigated,
found the cause, and it turned out to be an application change -- so it went
to Mark, and he fixed it. Nobody argued about whose job it was. That is what
we told you we would do.

Call me if you want to talk through any of this, or if your venues are
asking questions I can help you answer.
