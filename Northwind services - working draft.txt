NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
Services -- working draft

Prepared by:  John Mercer
Reviewed by:  Alice Chen
Date:         March 22, 2010
Status:       Working draft. Not for customer distribution.


PURPOSE

Notes for conversations with prospects. I will be using this when I start
calling contacts over the next several weeks. It is not a proposal, a price
list, or a contract, and nothing in it should be handed to anyone outside
the company.


WHO WE ARE

Northwind Technologies. Denver, Colorado. Founded this month by John Mercer
and Alice Chen.

Two people. We lease space and buy equipment as customer work justifies it.
We do not own a datacenter and are not trying to.


WHO WE THINK WE ARE FOR

Companies that need dedicated infrastructure and real operational support
but are too small, or too busy, to build an infrastructure team of their
own.

They have outgrown shared hosting or a single server in a closet. They are
not big enough to hire two or three systems administrators. They have
production systems that matter to the business and nobody whose actual job
is keeping those systems running.

In my experience these companies are usually already paying somebody for
rack space, bandwidth, or servers, and finding that nobody is responsible
for whether the whole thing works.


WHAT WE DO

Alice and I went back and forth on how to say this.

Short version, for a first conversation:

    We run your infrastructure so you don't have to hire someone to do it.

Work we expect to take on:

    - Dedicated server hosting
    - Linux systems administration
    - Network configuration and management
    - Storage and backup
    - Monitoring and incident response
    - System provisioning and deployment
    - Colocation support
    - Migrations and infrastructure projects

That list is broad on purpose. What any particular customer buys is going to
depend on what they already have and what they actually need.


WHERE OUR RESPONSIBILITY ENDS

Alice's concern, and she is right about it: if we tell customers we take
responsibility for "the whole environment," we will end up owning problems
we did not price, cannot see, and cannot fix.

What we can be responsible for:

    - Systems we built, or systems we have inventoried and understand
    - Infrastructure: hardware, operating system, network, storage, backup
    - Monitoring the things we have agreed to monitor

What we are not taking responsibility for, unless we agree to it
specifically and in writing:

    - Customer application code
    - Databases we did not build and have not been given time to understand
    - Third-party vendors and services we do not control
    - Anything on a system we have not been given full access to

Before we take on an environment somebody else built, we look at it first.
We are not quoting sight unseen. Alice was firm about this and I don't think
it costs us anything in a first conversation.


HOW WE ARE DIFFERENT

Most providers sell rack space, bandwidth, servers, or a fixed managed
services package. When something breaks in the gaps between those things it
is the customer's problem to figure out.

We are proposing to be the people who care whether the whole thing works.

Whether customers will pay a premium for that is what I am going to find out
over the next few months.


WHAT WE HAVE NOT DECIDED

These came up while writing this and we did not settle them. I should avoid
committing to any of them in a first conversation.

    Pricing. Monthly per server, hourly, retainer, or some mix. We do not
    have a rate. I can talk about what we do without talking about what it
    costs, at least for the first call.

    Response times and availability. We have not agreed on what we will
    promise. Two people cannot promise what a large provider promises.
    Alice does not want a number written down until we know what we can
    actually staff, and I think that is correct even though it is going to
    come up early.

    After hours. Related to the above. Right now this means "call Alice,"
    which is not a plan.

    Contract terms. We do not have a services agreement. We will need one
    before we sign anybody.

    Colocation. We have not picked a facility. We should get quotes from
    the Denver providers so I can talk about pricing intelligently, but we
    are not leasing space before there is a customer to put in it.

    Hardware. Whether customers buy their own, or we buy and they lease, or
    some mix. Affects pricing and affects what we can say about replacement.

    Geography. How far outside Denver we are willing to go for work that
    needs hands on site.

    Whether we take on environments we would not have built. We probably
    have to, at first. See the look-first rule above.
