NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
mon1 -- the night it filled up

Prepared by:  Toby Whelan
Reviewed by:  Priya Raghunathan
Date:         20 February 2017
Status:       Internal


WHAT HAPPENED

Alice's workstation mailed her at 04:51 on Saturday saying mon1 had not reported
in for twenty minutes.  She rang me at 05:10 and I was on it by half past.

mon1's disk was full.  nwcheck could not write its state files, could not write
the heartbeat, and had been failing quietly since about 02:00.

    For roughly three hours nothing was monitoring anything, for any customer,
    and the only reason we found out is a check Alice wrote in May 2011 that
    runs on her own workstation and that she has described twice in writing as
    a partial fix.

It caught it.  I want that said before the rest of this, because everything else
in here is about things we got wrong and that one we got right six years ago.


WHAT FILLED IT

/var/lib/nwcheck/history.

Priya added it in December 2012 after the Summit on-sale, when eleven warnings
had arrived one at a time over two years and nobody had ever seen them together.
One line per check per run.  She wrote in the code that it is not a graph and
not a platform, it is a file you can grep.

It was right then and it is still the right idea.  The arithmetic has just moved
underneath it.

    Checks in nwcheck.conf, December 2012      31
    Checks in nwcheck.conf, this morning       67
    Runs per day                              288
    Days since December 2012                1,536

I did the obvious multiplication first and got twenty-nine and a half million,
and then remembered that we did not have sixty-seven checks in 2013.  We had
thirty-one until Gary's rigs arrived, forty-two until the suite, and we lost
eleven again when Gary left.  Sixty-seven is five months old.

So I stopped calculating and counted.

    wc -l                              18,412,006
    du                                       1.6 GB

That is eighteen million lines and 1.6 gigabytes on a virtual server with a
twenty gigabyte disk that also holds every customer record, the customer-scripts
repository and Hartwell & Reed's nightly backups.

    A note on the thirty-one, because I got it wrong twice before I got it
    right.  Counting the lines above the Suite C block gives thirty-two, not
    thirty-one.  The extra one is the /checkout check on bw-web1, which Priya
    added in November 2016 after the vendor broke Brightwater's checkout page,
    and which lives inside the Brightwater block because that is where
    Brightwater's checks go.

    The config is grouped by customer and not by date, so you cannot count
    backwards through it.  I only noticed because the arithmetic came out wrong
    against Priya's own note from May 2011, which says thirty-one.

Nobody did anything wrong.  The file has been growing for four years, faster
each time we took a customer, and it crossed the line at two o'clock on a
Saturday morning.


THE PART THAT IS EMBARRASSING

mon1 is not in nwcheck.conf.

It watches sixty-seven things across eight customers and a suite, and it does not
watch itself.  There is no disk check on mon1.  We have had a disk check since
May 2010 and it has fired on six different customer machines and it has never
once been pointed at the machine that runs it.

    Priya asked in April 2011 what alerts us if mon1 stops.  The answer then was
    nothing, and we built the heartbeat.  The heartbeat tells us mon1 has
    stopped.  It does not tell us mon1 is about to.


WHAT I DID AT SIX IN THE MORNING

Compressed the history in place, which bought 1.6 gigabytes and took eleven
minutes.  Started nwcheck.  Everything came back.  No customer noticed and
nobody rang us.

I did not delete anything.  Priya's rule about not deleting things you do not
understand has been on the wall since before I started.


WHAT WE HAVE DONE SINCE

**Rotation.**  history rotates monthly and gzips.  nwtrend reads the current
file and, if you ask it to, the archives as well.  It is slower against the
archives and that is fine because nobody looks at 2014 in a hurry.

    The old file is not being converted to anything.  The archives are the
    same format they always were.  If somebody wants a proper time series in
    five years they can have this argument then.

**A bigger mon1.**  Forty dollars a month instead of twenty-eight, and eighty
gigabytes instead of twenty.  Marcus's comment: "We have been running the thing
that watches four hundred thousand dollars of customer commitment on a virtual
server that costs less than my telephone."

He is right and it has been true since 2010 and none of us noticed because it
kept working.

**mon1 watches mon1.**  Disk, and the heartbeat stays.  Six years late.


WHAT WE ARE NOT DOING

Replacing nwcheck.  Priya was clear about this and I agree with her.

It runs sixty-seven checks across eight customers, it has caught real failures
including one that had been silently broken for four nights, and the only thing
wrong with it this week was a disk.  Rewriting it because the company is bigger
is how you spend three months and end up with something that does the same thing
and is new.

Not touching the records, not touching nwpush, not moving anything to a
database.  One file rotated, one machine resized, one check added.
