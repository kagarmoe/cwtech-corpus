NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
Everything we learned is upside down

Prepared by:  Priya Raghunathan
Reviewed by:  Alice Chen, Marcus Reyes
Date:         19 October 2015
Status:       Internal

Two of the eleven are still talking to us.  I have spent six weeks finding out
what they actually need and it is not what I expected, so I am writing it down
before we design anything.


THE TWO

**Dr Ma**, the genomics core at the university.  Sequencers producing more data
than she can process.  Some of her analysis runs a great deal faster on GPUs.
She cannot buy hardware because a capital request takes eleven months and
usually fails.

**Dev Arora**, Sightline Post.  Twelve people, commercials and corporate video.
Eight workstations that render overnight, where overnight is sometimes four
hours because a client moved a deadline.


WHAT THEY HAVE IN COMMON

They want dense power and cooling, and they do not want to own the machines.

That is the entire overlap.


WHERE MINING TAUGHT US THE WRONG LESSONS

This is the part I want on the record, because for two and a half years we have
been getting very good at a set of assumptions that are all false here.

    **Mining:** flat load, forever.
    **These two:** Dr Ma runs for four days and then nothing for two weeks.
    Dev is dead in March and on fire in February.  Marcus designed our cooling
    for a load that never varies.  A room that swings from 20 kW to 140 kW and
    back is a different thermal problem and he says it is a harder one.

    **Mining:** no data.  A dead machine is unplugged and replaced.
    **These two:** the data is the entire point.  Dr Ma's runs are irreplaceable
    for the fortnight it would take to redo them, and Dev's rushes are
    irreplaceable full stop.  If we lose a machine mid-render for Dev we have
    not lost a machine, we have lost his week.

    **Mining:** nobody is waiting.
    **These two:** somebody is always waiting, and for Dev the waiting is
    contractual.

    **Mining:** bandwidth is trivial.
    **These two:** Dev has to get four hundred gigabytes in and eighty out, per
    job, and the getting it there is the part he complains about first.

    **Mining:** we do not understand the workload and do not need to.
    **These two:** we still do not need to understand it — but we need to know
    when it has failed, and neither of them can tell us that from outside.  A
    render that dies at 60 per cent looks exactly like a render that is at 60
    per cent.

So the thing we have got good at — watching liveness, power and temperature
while knowing nothing — covers about a third of what these two need.


WHAT ACTUALLY TRANSFERS

The building capability transfers completely.  Dense power, containment,
cooling, remote hands, dealing with a facility.  That is real and it is four
years of work and neither of them can buy it anywhere convenient.

The operating model does not transfer at all.  What these two need is closer to
what we do for Summit than what we did for Halvard: know what is on the machine,
notice when it broke, have an opinion about backups, answer the telephone to
somebody with a deadline.

    **We did not learn a new business in the suite.  We learned a new building.
    The business we need is the one we already had.**

I have been trying to write that sentence for a month and I want to be careful
that it is not too neat.  It is not entirely true — remote hands is genuinely
new, and so is operating at this density, and so is Marcus.  But the shape of
the customer relationship is our old shape and we spent three years thinking we
had moved on from it.


THE THING NOBODY HAS AN ANSWER TO

Neither of them will buy hardware.

Dr Ma cannot.  Dev could and will not, because his utilisation is 15 per cent
and he knows it.

Every customer this company has ever had has owned their own machines or leased
them from a facility.  We have never owned hardware.  Alice has said several
times, in writing, that this is why the bad year was uncomfortable rather than
fatal.

If we want either of these customers we have to answer a question we have
avoided since 2010, and I do not think it is my question to answer.


WHAT I SUGGEST

One of them, small, paid, for three months, on hardware we do not buy.

Dev has four machines he would ship us tomorrow and a February coming.  Let us
find out whether we can run a bursty, data-carrying, deadline-driven workload in
that suite before we decide what any of this is.

Not a product.  A trial with an end date.
