CAIRNWELL TECHNOLOGIES
The hardware question, answered

Prepared by:  Alice Chen
Reviewed by:  John Mercer, Marcus Reyes, Priya Raghunathan
Date:         16 May 2016
Status:       Internal


THE QUESTION

Since 2010 we have owned no hardware of consequence.  I have written down three
times that this is why our bad year was uncomfortable and not fatal, and I meant
it every time.

Since October Priya has twice said the hardware question was not hers to answer,
which was a polite way of saying it was mine.

Dev wants twelve machines and will not buy them.  Dr Ma cannot buy them.  Two
more people have asked the same thing since February.  That is four, which is
not opportunistic any more.


WHAT I HAVE DECIDED

We buy hardware, under the same rule we already use for power.

    **We do not buy beyond the contracts behind it.**

That is the rule John and I agreed in November 2013 about the cage and it has
held for three years through the worst of this.  I am not inventing a new
principle.  I am applying the one that worked to the thing we kept saying no to.

In practice: a machine gets bought when a customer has signed a term that covers
its cost, with margin, and not before.  Nobody speculates on a rack of GPUs
because somebody might want them.

    First tranche: fourteen nodes against Dev's twelve-machine autumn commitment
    and Dr Ma's grant, which runs to September 2018.


WHAT MADE THIS AFFORDABLE, WHICH IS NOT A COMFORTABLE STORY

The GPU market is full of second-hand cards from people who were mining and
stopped.

We paid about forty per cent of new for hardware that is two years old and has
been run flat out in somebody's garage, and Marcus tested every one of them for
a week before it went in a rack.  Eleven of the sixty cards we bought were dead
or dying.  We knew that going in and priced for it.

In 2014 Gary Lindqvist asked whether we would buy his rigs and we said no.  That
was the right answer in 2014 — we had no customer for them and no reason to own
anything.

It is a strange feeling to build the next part of the company out of the wreckage
of the last part, and I would rather write that down than pretend the symmetry
has not occurred to me.


WHAT WE ARE NOT DOING

**Not renting compute by the hour.**  Several people have asked.  That is a
different business with a different billing system and a different kind of
customer, and we have neither.

**Not buying ahead of demand.**  The whole rule.

**Not touching the customer's work.**  Same line we have held since April 2010.
We run the machine.  What it computes is theirs.

**Not renaming anything.**  See below, because Priya was firm about it.


THE THING PRIYA WAS FIRM ABOUT

Our check for whether a machine is doing work is called `rig_alive`.  It was
written in June 2013 for Gary's mining rigs.  It now runs against Dev's render
nodes and Dr Ma's analysis boxes.

I suggested renaming it.  Priya said no, and her reasons were better than mine:

    It would be an afternoon of work across four customers' configs to change a
    word, and every one of those edits is a chance to break something that
    currently works.

    Anybody reading the file learns something true from the old name — that we
    got into this through mining, and that the thing we check has not actually
    changed.

    "We would be tidying the history to make ourselves look like we planned
    this."

So it stays `rig_alive`.  The config still has a section header that says
Lindqvist (mining) with nothing at all under it.  Gary has been gone since
January 2015 and Marcus left the header there deliberately when he moved the
last of it, with a note underneath saying that this is where the check came
from and where we learned to watch a room.

An empty heading in a config file is a strange thing to keep on purpose.  It is
also the only place in that file that records where any of this started.


WHAT THIS COSTS US IF I AM WRONG

Forty-one thousand dollars of hardware against contracts worth about ninety
over their terms.

If both customers vanished tomorrow we would have a rack of two-year-old GPUs
and a suite with room in it.  That is a bad month, not a bad year, and it is the
first time since 2013 that I can write a sentence like that about a decision of
mine without checking it twice.
